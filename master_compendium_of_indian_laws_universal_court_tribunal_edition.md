# PART I: GENERAL CRIMINAL LAW FRAMEWORK

## 1. BHARATIYA NYAYA SANHITA (BNS), 2023
*(Substantive Criminal Law — 20 Chapters, 358 Sections; Replaces the Indian Penal Code, 1860)*

### 1.1 Preliminary, Jurisdiction & Definitions (Sections 1–3)
* **Intra-Territorial Jurisdiction (Section 1(3)):** Every person—regardless of nationality or citizenship—is liable to punishment under the BNS for any act or omission contrary to its provisions committed within the territorial boundaries of India (including territorial waters up to 12 nautical miles and airspace above).
* **Extra-Territorial Jurisdiction (Section 1(4)):** Indian courts have jurisdiction over offences committed outside India by:
  * Any citizen of India in any place beyond India.
  * Any person on any ship or aircraft registered in India, wherever it may be located.
  * Any person in any place without and beyond India committing an offence targeting a computer resource located in India.
* **Savings of Special and Local Laws (Section 1(5)):** The BNS does not repeal, vary, suspend, or affect any Special Law (applicable to a particular subject, e.g., NDPS Act, POCSO Act) or Local Law (applicable to a particular part of India).
* **Key Statutory Definitions (Section 2):**
  * **Child (Sec. 2(3)):** Uniformly defined as any person below the age of 18 years.
  * **Document (Sec. 2(8)):** Explicitly expanded to include electronic and digital records alongside paper writings, maps, and inscriptions.
  * **Gender (Sec. 2(10)):** The pronoun "he" and its derivatives apply to any person, whether male, female, or transgender.
  * **Public Servant (Sec. 2(28)):** Encompasses 12 distinct categories, including judges, commissioned military officers, government officers whose duty is to prevent offences or protect public health/safety, municipal commissioners, and arbitrators.
* **Common Intention (Section 3(5)):** When a criminal act is done by several persons in furtherance of the common intention of all, each of such persons is liable for that act in the same manner as if it were done by him alone. Requires a pre-arranged plan and active participation.

### 1.2 Theory & Types of Punishments (Sections 4–13)
* **Six Categories of Punishment (Section 4):**
  1. **Death Penalty:** Awarded strictly in the "rarest of rare" cases (e.g., aggravated murder, gang rape of a child under 12, terrorist acts resulting in death).
  2. **Imprisonment for Life:** Means rigorous imprisonment for the remainder of the convict's natural biological life unless commuted or remitted by the appropriate Government.
  3. **Imprisonment:** Divided into *Rigorous* (with hard labour) and *Simple* (without hard labour).
  4. **Forfeiture of Property:** Confiscation of specific assets used in or derived from crime.
  5. **Fine:** Monetary penalty imposed either as a sole punishment or alongside imprisonment.
  6. **Community Service:** Newly introduced reformative punishment (defined under BNSS Sec. 23 as unpaid work benefiting the community, applicable to petty offences like first-time theft under ₹5,000, defamation, public intoxication, and attempt to commit suicide to compel/restrain public servants).
* **Commutation of Sentence (Section 5):** The appropriate Government may, without the consent of the offender, commute a death sentence to any other punishment, or life imprisonment to imprisonment not exceeding 14 years (subject to BNSS Sec. 475 restrictions).
* **Default of Payment of Fine (Section 8):**
  * Where an offence is punishable with imprisonment as well as fine, the default imprisonment cannot exceed one-fourth of the maximum term fixed for the offence.
  * Where an offence is punishable with fine only, the default imprisonment must be simple and follows a graded scale: up to 2 months (fine $\le$ ₹5,000), up to 4 months (fine $\le$ ₹10,000), and up to 1 year (in any other case).
* **Solitary Confinement (Sections 11–12):** Can only be ordered for offences punishable with rigorous imprisonment; cannot exceed 3 months in total, and no single spell may exceed 14 days at a time.

### 1.3 General Exceptions / Criminal Defences (Sections 14–44)
* **Mistake of Fact (Sections 14 & 17):** Nothing is an offence done by a person who is, or by reason of a mistake of fact (not mistake of law) in good faith believes himself to be, bound by law or justified by law in doing it (*Ignorantia facti excusat, ignorantia juris non excusat*).
* **Judicial Acts (Sections 15–16):** Absolute immunity for judges acting judicially within jurisdiction, and ministerial officers executing valid court orders.
* **Accident (Section 18):** An act done by accident or misfortune, without criminal intention or knowledge, in the doing of a lawful act in a lawful manner by lawful means and with proper care and caution.
* **Doctrine of Necessity (Section 19):** An act likely to cause harm, done without criminal intent and in good faith, for the purpose of preventing or avoiding other greater harm to person or property.
* **Infancy (Sections 20–21):**
  * *Under 7 years (Sec. 20):* Absolute immunity (*Doli Incapax*); irrebuttable presumption that a child lacks criminal capacity.
  * *Between 7 and 12 years (Sec. 21):* Qualified immunity; excused only if the child has not attained sufficient maturity of understanding to judge the nature and consequences of the conduct.
* **Unsoundness of Mind (Section 22):** Based on the McNaghten Rules. Excuses an act if, at the time of commission, the accused by reason of unsoundness of mind was incapable of knowing the nature of the act, or that what he was doing was either wrong or contrary to law.
* **Intoxication (Sections 23–24):**
  * *Involuntary intoxication (Sec. 23):* Valid defence if the intoxicant was administered without the person's knowledge or against their will, impairing cognitive judgment.
  * *Voluntary intoxication (Sec. 24):* Not a defence; the person is presumed to have the same knowledge as if sober, though specific intent may be evaluated.
* **Consent & Good Faith Acts (Sections 25–30):** Harm caused by consent (excluding death or grievous hurt, except in lawful sports or medical surgeries performed in good faith for the patient's benefit).
* **Duress / Compulsion by Threats (Section 32):** Excuses an act compelled by threats of instant death, *except* murder and offences against the State punishable with death.
* **Trivial Acts (Section 33):** Nothing is an offence if the harm caused is so slight that no person of ordinary sense and temper would complain (*De minimis non curat lex*).
* **Right of Private Defence (Sections 34–44):**
  * Available against any offence affecting the human body or property (theft, robbery, mischief, criminal trespass).
  * No right of private defence against a public servant acting in good faith under colour of office unless the act causes reasonable apprehension of death or grievous hurt (Sec. 37).
  * **Causing Death in Defence of Body (Sec. 38):** Permitted against 7 specific assaults: (1) apprehension of death, (2) apprehension of grievous hurt, (3) intention of committing rape, (4) intention of gratifying unnatural lust, (5) intention of kidnapping or abducting, (6) wrongful confinement where recourse to public authorities is impossible, (7) act of throwing or administering acid.
  * **Causing Death in Defence of Property (Sec. 41):** Permitted against: (1) robbery, (2) house-breaking after sunset and before sunrise, (3) mischief by fire/explosive on a dwelling house or vessel, (4) theft, mischief, or house-trespass under circumstances causing reasonable apprehension of death or grievous hurt.

### 1.4 Inchoate Offences: Abetment, Conspiracy & Attempt (Sections 45–62)
* **Abetment (Sections 45–60):** Consists of three modes: (a) instigating any person to do a thing, (b) engaging in a conspiracy to do it, or (c) intentionally aiding by any act or illegal omission. Section 48 specifically criminalizes abetment in India of offences committed outside India.
* **Criminal Conspiracy (Section 61):**
  * *Definition (Sec. 61(1)):* When two or more persons agree to do, or cause to be done, an illegal act, or a legal act by illegal means.
  * For a conspiracy to commit an offence, the mere agreement constitutes the crime; for other illegal acts, an overt act in pursuance of the agreement is required.
* **Attempt (Section 62):** Punishes attempts to commit offences punishable with life imprisonment or imprisonment where no express provision is made for the punishment of such attempt (punishable up to one-half of the longest term of imprisonment provided for the offence).

### 1.5 Offences Against Woman and Child (Sections 63–99)
* **Rape (Sections 63–66):**
  * Defined under 7 clauses covering non-consensual sexual acts, consent obtained by fear of death/hurt, misconception of fact, unsoundness of mind/intoxication, or when the woman is under 18 years of age (statutory rape).
  * *Punishment (Sec. 64):* Rigorous imprisonment of not less than 10 years, extending to life imprisonment, and fine.
  * *Aggravated Rape (Sec. 65):* Rape of a woman under 16 years carries minimum 20 years to life; under 12 years carries minimum 20 years to life or death.
* **Sexual Intercourse by Deceitful Means (Section 69):** Criminalizes obtaining consent for sexual intercourse by deceitful means—explicitly including making a false promise to marry without any intention of fulfilling it, or falsely promising employment/promotion/inducement—punishable with imprisonment up to 10 years and fine.
* **Gang Rape (Section 70):** All persons acting in furtherance of common intention are deemed to have committed rape (minimum 20 years to life; if victim is under 18, life imprisonment for remainder of natural life or death).
* **Outraging Modesty & Sexual Harassment (Sections 74–78):**
  * *Sec. 74:* Assault/criminal force to outrage modesty (1 to 5 years).
  * *Sec. 75:* Sexual harassment (unwelcome physical contact, demand for sexual favours, showing pornography, sexually coloured remarks).
  * *Sec. 76:* Assault with intent to disrobe (3 to 7 years).
  * *Sec. 77:* Voyeurism (watching/capturing image of woman engaging in private act).
  * *Sec. 78:* Stalking (following a woman repeatedly or monitoring her use of internet, email, or electronic communication despite clear indication of disinterest).
* **Dowry Death & Cruelty (Sections 80, 85–86):**
  * *Dowry Death (Sec. 80):* Death of a woman caused by burns, bodily injury, or unnatural circumstances within 7 years of marriage, where it is shown that soon before her death she was subjected to cruelty or harassment by her husband or his relatives for/in connection with any demand for dowry (minimum 7 years to life).
  * *Cruelty (Secs. 85–86):* Wilful conduct likely to drive a woman to suicide or cause grave injury to life/limb/health (mental or physical), or harassment to coerce unlawful dowry demands (up to 3 years).
* **Offences Against Children (Sections 87–99):** Covers abandonment of child under 12 (Sec. 93), hiring/employing a child to commit an offence (Sec. 95 — punishable identically to having committed the offence directly), and procurement/importation of children (Secs. 96–97). *(Note: Sexual offences against children under 18 are additionally governed by the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences [POCSO] Act, 2012, tried before Special POCSO Courts).*

### 1.6 Offences Affecting the Human Body (Sections 100–146)
* **Culpable Homicide vs. Murder (Sections 100–105):**
  * *Culpable Homicide (Sec. 100):* Causing death by doing an act with the intention of causing death, or with the intention of causing such bodily injury as is likely to cause death, or with the knowledge that the act is likely to cause death.
  * *Murder (Sec. 101):* Culpable homicide is murder if the act falls within 4 aggravated clauses of intention/knowledge, unless it falls within the 5 mitigating Exceptions (grave and sudden provocation, exceeding right of private defence in good faith, public servant exceeding powers in good faith, sudden fight without premeditation, or consent of victim above 18).
  * *Mob Lynching (Sec. 103(2)):* When a group of 5 or more persons acting in concert commits murder on the ground of race, caste, community, sex, place of birth, language, personal belief, or any similar ground, every member is punishable with death or life imprisonment, and fine.
* **Causing Death by Negligence & Hit-and-Run (Section 106):**
  * *Sec. 106(1):* Rash or negligent act not amounting to culpable homicide is punishable up to 5 years (reduced to 2 years for registered medical practitioners performing a medical procedure).
  * *Sec. 106(2):* Causing death by rash and negligent driving of a vehicle and escaping without reporting it to a police officer or Magistrate soon after the incident carries imprisonment up to 10 years and fine.
* **Organised Crime & Petty Organised Crime (Sections 111–112):**
  * *Organised Crime (Sec. 111):* Any continuing unlawful activity (kidnapping, robbery, vehicle theft, extortion, land grabbing, contract killing, economic offences, cyber-crimes, trafficking) carried out by an individual or syndicate using violence, threat, intimidation, or coercion.
  * *Petty Organised Crime (Sec. 112):* Pickpocketing, snatching, shoplifting, card skimming, ATM theft, or selling unauthorized tickets committed by a group or gang.
* **Terrorist Act (Section 113):** Any act committed with intent to threaten the unity, integrity, sovereignty, security, or economic security of India, or to strike terror in the people, using bombs, dynamite, firearms, hazardous substances, or disrupting essential supplies/critical infrastructure.
* **Hurt, Grievous Hurt & Acid Attack (Sections 114–125):**
  * *Grievous Hurt (Sec. 116):* Comprises 8 specific injuries: emasculation, permanent privation of sight of either eye, permanent privation of hearing of either ear, privation of any member/joint, destruction/permanent impairing of powers of any member/joint, permanent disfiguration of head/face, fracture or dislocation of a bone or tooth, and any hurt endangering life or causing severe bodily pain/inability to follow ordinary pursuits for 15 days.
  * *Acid Attack (Sec. 124):* Voluntarily causing grievous hurt by acid carries minimum 10 years to life imprisonment; mere throwing or attempting to throw acid carries 5 to 7 years (Sec. 124(2)).
* **Kidnapping, Abduction & Trafficking (Sections 137–146):**
  * *Kidnapping (Sec. 137):* Two types—kidnapping from India (beyond limits of India without consent) and kidnapping from lawful guardianship (taking/enticing a child under 18 or person of unsound mind out of lawful guardian's keeping).
  * *Abduction (Sec. 138):* Compelling by force or inducing by deceitful means any person to go from any place.
  * *Trafficking of Persons (Sec. 143):* Recruiting, transporting, harbouring, transferring, or receiving a person by threat, force, abduction, fraud, deception, abuse of power, or inducement for exploitation (prostitution, forced labour, slavery, organ removal).

### 1.7 Offences Against the State, Public Tranquillity & Property (Sections 147–358)
* **Acts Endangering Sovereignty, Unity and Integrity of India (Section 152):** Replaces colonial sedition. Punishes anyone who purposely or knowingly—by words, signs, visible representation, electronic communication, or use of financial means—excites or attempts to excite secession, armed rebellion, or subversive activities, or encourages feelings of separatist activities (imprisonment for life or up to 7 years, and fine).
* **Unlawful Assembly & Rioting (Sections 189–191):**
  * *Unlawful Assembly (Sec. 189):* Assembly of 5 or more persons with a common object to overawe the Government, resist execution of law, commit mischief/criminal trespass, or force a person to do what they are not legally bound to do.
  * *Rioting (Sec. 191):* Whenever force or violence is used by an unlawful assembly, or by any member thereof, in prosecution of the common object, every member is guilty of rioting.
* **Offences Against Property (Sections 303–334):**
  * *Theft (Sec. 303):* Dishonestly taking any movable property out of the possession of any person without that person's consent. First-time conviction for theft of property valued below ₹5,000 is punishable with community service upon return/restoration of property.
  * *Snatching (Sec. 304):* Suddenly, quickly, or forcibly seizing, securing, or grabbing any movable property from any person or from their possession (punishable up to 3 years).
  * *Extortion (Sec. 308):* Intentionally putting any person in fear of any injury and thereby dishonestly inducing them to deliver any property or valuable security.
  * *Robbery & Dacoity (Secs. 309–310):* Theft or extortion becomes *Robbery* when instant death, instant hurt, or instant wrongful restraint is caused or threatened. When 5 or more persons conjointly commit or attempt robbery, it becomes *Dacoity*.
  * *Criminal Breach of Trust (Sec. 316):* Dishonest misappropriation or conversion to one's own use of property entrusted to a person or over which they have dominion.
  * *Cheating (Sec. 318):* Fraudulently or dishonestly inducing a deceived person to deliver property or consent to retention of property, or intentionally inducing them to do/omit something they would not do if not deceived, causing damage/harm to body, mind, reputation, or property.
* **Forgery & Defamation (Sections 336, 356):**
  * *Forgery (Sec. 336):* Making a false document or electronic record with intent to cause damage/injury, support a claim/title, or commit fraud.
  * *Defamation (Sec. 356):* Making or publishing any imputation concerning any person intending to harm, or knowing/having reason to believe it will harm, the reputation of such person (subject to 10 statutory Exceptions including truth for public good, fair comment on public conduct, and accurate court reports).

## 2. BHARATIYA NAGARIK SURAKSHA SANHITA (BNSS), 2023
*(Procedural Criminal Law — 39 Chapters, 531 Sections; Replaces the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973)*

### 2.1 Digital Modernization & Court Hierarchy (Sections 1–29)
* **Audio-Video Electronic Means (Section 2(1)(a)):** Legally validates video conferencing and electronic recording for recording evidence, producing accused before Magistrates, conducting searches/seizures, and serving summons.
* **Criminal Court Hierarchy & Sentencing Powers (Sections 6, 21–23):**
  * *Supreme Court & High Court:* Any sentence authorized by law.
  * *Sessions Judge / Additional Sessions Judge:* Any sentence authorized by law, but a death sentence requires confirmation by the High Court.
  * *Chief Judicial Magistrate (CJM):* Imprisonment up to 7 years and/or fine.
  * *Judicial Magistrate of First Class (JMFC):* Imprisonment up to 3 years, fine up to ₹50,000, or community service.
  * *Judicial Magistrate of Second Class (JMSC):* Imprisonment up to 1 year, fine up to ₹10,000, or community service.

### 2.2 Arrest Protocols & Safeguards (Sections 35–62)
* **Arrest Without Warrant & Notice of Appearance (Section 35):**
  * Police may arrest without warrant in cognizable offences punishable with more than 7 years, or under 7 years if specific necessity conditions exist (to prevent further offences, ensure proper investigation, prevent tampering with evidence, or ensure court attendance).
  * *Notice of Appearance (Sec. 35(3)):* Where arrest is not required for offences punishable up to 7 years, the police must issue a written Notice of Appearance. If the person complies, they cannot be arrested unless reasons are recorded.
  * *Elderly & Infirm Safeguard (Sec. 35(7)):* No arrest shall be made without prior permission of an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police (DySP) in offences punishable with less than 3 years if the person is infirm or above 60 years of age.
* **Transparency & Rights of Arrested Persons (Sections 36, 37, 47, 48, 58):**
  * Police officer must bear accurate, visible, and clear identification and prepare an arrest memo attested by a witness (family member or respectable local resident) and countersigned by the arrestee (Sec. 36).
  * Every police station and district headquarters must designate a police officer to maintain and prominently display (physically and digitally) the names and addresses of all arrested persons and the nature of their offences (Sec. 37).
  * Right to be informed of full particulars/grounds of arrest and right to bail (Sec. 47); obligation of police to immediately inform a nominated relative or friend (Sec. 48).
  * Mandatory production before the nearest Magistrate within **24 hours** of arrest, excluding journey time (Sec. 58).
* **Use of Handcuffs (Section 43(3)):** Strictly restricted to habitual/repeat offenders, escaped convicts, or persons accused of organized crime, terrorist acts, drug offences, illegal arms, murder, rape, acid attack, counterfeiting, economic offences, or offences against the State.

### 2.3 Investigation, FIR, Forensics & Police Custody (Sections 173–196)
* **Zero FIR & e-FIR (Section 173):**
  * Every officer in charge of a police station must register an FIR relating to a cognizable offence **irrespective of the area where the offence is committed (Zero FIR)**, and subsequently transfer it to the jurisdictional station.
  * Information may be given orally or via **electronic communication (e-FIR)**, which is taken on record upon being signed by the informant within 3 days.
  * *Preliminary Inquiry (Sec. 173(3)):* For cognizable offences punishable between 3 and 7 years, the officer may—with prior permission of a DySP—conduct a preliminary inquiry within 14 days to ascertain whether a *prima facie* case exists.
* **Mandatory Forensic Investigation (Section 176(3)):** For every offence punishable with **7 years or more**, the officer in charge must cause a forensic expert to visit the crime scene to collect forensic evidence and record the process on a mobile phone or other electronic device.
* **Mandatory Audio-Video Search & Seizure (Section 105):** Every search and seizure operation (and preparation of the list of seized items) must be recorded through audio-video electronic means and forwarded without delay to the District Magistrate, Sub-divisional Magistrate, or Judicial Magistrate.
* **Police Custody & Default Bail (Section 187):**
  * A Magistrate may authorize police custody for a maximum total period of **15 days**, which can be availed either in whole or in parts at any time during the initial **40 days** (where investigation period is 60 days) or initial **60 days** (where investigation period is 90 days).
  * *Default / Statutory Bail (Sec. 187(3)):* If the charge-sheet is not filed within **90 days** (for offences punishable with death, life imprisonment, or 10+ years) or **60 days** (for all other offences), the accused acquires an indefeasible statutory right to be released on bail.
* **Charge-Sheet & Further Investigation Timelines (Section 193):**
  * Police must inform the informant or victim of the progress of investigation within **90 days** (including by electronic communication).
  * Further investigation after filing the charge-sheet during trial requires permission of the Court and must be completed within **90 days** (extendable by the Court).

### 2.4 Trial Timelines, In Absentia Trial & Bail (Sections 218–496)
* **Sanction for Prosecution of Public Servants (Section 218):** Government must decide on granting or refusing prosecution sanction within **120 days** from receipt of request; if it fails to do so, sanction is **deemed to have been accorded**.
* **Right of Accused to be Heard Before Cognizance (Section 223):** In complaint cases before a Magistrate, no cognizance of an offence shall be taken without giving the accused an opportunity of being heard.
* **Strict Trial Timelines:**
  * Discharge application to be preferred within **60 days** from supply of copies (Sec. 250 / 262).
  * Framing of charges within **60 days** from the first hearing on charge (Sec. 251 / 263).
  * Plea bargaining application to be filed within **30 days** from framing of charge (Sec. 290).
  * Judgment to be pronounced within **30 days** (extendable up to **45 days** for recorded reasons) after termination of trial (Sec. 392).
* **Trial and Judgment in Absentia (Section 356):** If a proclaimed offender has absconded to evade trial and there is no immediate prospect of arresting him, the Court—after issuing two consecutive arrest warrants, publishing a proclamation, and waiting **90 days** from framing charges—may proceed with the trial and pronounce judgment in his absence as if he were present.
* **Bail for First-Time Undertrials (Section 479):**
  * General rule: An undertrial prisoner who has undergone detention for up to **one-half** of the maximum period of imprisonment specified for that offence (excluding offences punishable with death or life) shall be released on bail.
  * *New Reform:* A **first-time offender** (never convicted of any offence in the past) shall be released on bond by the Court if they have undergone detention for up to **one-third** of the maximum period of imprisonment.

## 3. BHARATIYA SAKSHYA ADHINIYAM (BSA), 2023
*(Law of Evidence — 4 Parts, 12 Chapters, 170 Sections; Replaces the Indian Evidence Act, 1872)*

### 3.1 Core Doctrines of Relevancy (Sections 1–50)
* **Facts in Issue & Relevant Facts (Section 3):** Evidence may be given in any suit or proceeding of the existence or non-existence of every fact in issue and of such other facts as are declared to be relevant under the Act, and of no others.
* **Res Gestae (Section 4):** Facts which, though not in issue, are so connected with a fact in issue as to form part of the same transaction (whether occurring at the same time and place or at different times and places) are relevant.
* **Motive, Preparation & Conduct (Section 6):** Any fact is relevant which shows or constitutes a motive or preparation for any fact in issue, or the previous/subsequent conduct of any party or victim influenced by the fact in issue.
* **Plea of Alibi (Section 9):** Facts not otherwise relevant become relevant if they are inconsistent with any fact in issue or make the existence of a fact in issue highly improbable (e.g., proving the accused was in another city at the exact time of the crime).
* **Confessions & Discovery Statements (Sections 22–24):**
  * A confession caused by inducement, threat, coercion, or promise is irrelevant in a criminal proceeding (Sec. 22).
  * No confession made to a police officer or while in police custody (unless in the immediate presence of a Magistrate) can be proved against the accused (Sec. 23(1) & (2)).
  * *Discovery Statement (Sec. 23(2) Proviso):* When any fact is deposed to as discovered in consequence of information received from a person accused of any offence in police custody, so much of such information as relates distinctly to the fact thereby discovered (e.g., weapon recovery) may be proved.
* **Dying Declaration (Section 26(a)):** Statements, written or verbal, made by a person as to the cause of his death, or as to any of the circumstances of the transaction which resulted in his death, are relevant when the cause of death comes into question—even if the person was not under expectation of death when making it.
* **Expert Opinion (Section 39):** Opinions of persons specially skilled in foreign law, science, art, handwriting, finger impressions, or **Examiners of Electronic Evidence** (Sec. 39(2)) are relevant facts.

### 3.2 Documentary & Electronic Evidence (Sections 54–93)
* **Primary vs. Secondary Evidence (Sections 57–58):**
  * *Primary Evidence (Sec. 57):* Means the original document itself produced for inspection. Explanations 4 to 7 establish that where an electronic/digital record is created or stored simultaneously or sequentially in multiple files, each file is primary evidence; where produced from proper custody, it is primary evidence unless disputed; where a video recording is simultaneously stored in electronic form and transmitted/broadcast, each stored recording is primary evidence.
  * *Secondary Evidence (Sec. 58):* Includes certified copies, copies made from the original by mechanical/electronic processes, counterparts, and oral/written admissions.
* **Legal Parity & Admissibility of Electronic Records (Sections 61–63):**
  * *Sec. 61:* Nothing in the Adhiniyam shall apply to deny the admissibility of an electronic or digital record on the ground that it is an electronic or digital record; it has the exact same legal effect, validity, and enforceability as any other document.
  * *Sec. 63 (Replaces Sec. 65B IEA):* Any information contained in an electronic record printed on paper, stored, recorded, or copied in optical/magnetic media or semiconductor memory is deemed a document and admissible without further proof of the original **only if accompanied by a mandatory two-part Certificate** (specified in the Schedule):
    * **Part A:** Signed by the person occupying a responsible official position in relation to the operation or management of the relevant device/communication device.
    * **Part B:** Signed by an expert certifying the hash value (e.g., SHA-256) and integrity of the electronic record.

### 3.3 Burden of Proof, Estoppel & Witnesses (Sections 104–168)
* **Burden of Proof (Sections 104–106):** Whoever desires any Court to give judgment as to any legal right or liability dependent on the existence of facts which he asserts must prove that those facts exist (Sec. 104). When any fact is especially within the knowledge of any person, the burden of proving that fact is upon him (Sec. 109).
* **Statutory Presumptions in Crimes Against Women (Sections 117, 118, 120):**
  * *Abetment of Suicide (Sec. 117):* If a married woman commits suicide within 7 years of marriage and was subjected to cruelty by her husband/his relatives, the Court *may presume* such suicide was abetted by them.
  * *Dowry Death (Sec. 118):* If a woman dies of dowry harassment within 7 years of marriage, the Court *shall presume* that the person caused the dowry death.
  * *Absence of Consent in Rape (Sec. 120):* In prosecutions for custodial or aggravated rape where sexual intercourse is proved and the woman states in her evidence that she did not consent, the Court *shall presume* she did not consent.
* **Estoppel (Section 121):** When one person has, by his declaration, act, or omission, intentionally caused or permitted another person to believe a thing to be true and to act upon such belief, neither he nor his representative shall be allowed to deny the truth of that thing in any suit or proceeding.
* **Examination of Witnesses (Sections 140–168):**
  * *Three Stages (Sec. 142):* Examination-in-chief (by party calling witness), Cross-examination (by adverse party), and Re-examination (to explain matters referred to in cross-examination).
  * *Leading Questions (Sec. 146):* Questions suggesting the desired answer; prohibited in examination-in-chief/re-examination (except on introductory/undisputed matters), but freely permitted in cross-examination.
  * *Hostile Witness (Sec. 157):* Court may permit the person who calls a witness to put cross-examination questions to their own witness if the witness turns adverse.

## 4. LANDMARK SUPREME COURT PRECEDENTS IN CRIMINAL LAW
* ***Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab*** **(1980) 2 SCC 684 — "Rarest of Rare" Doctrine:** Upheld the constitutional validity of the death penalty (now BNS Sec. 4 / Sec. 103) while establishing that capital punishment must be imposed only in the "rarest of rare" cases when the alternative option of life imprisonment is unquestionably foreclosed.
* ***D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal*** **(1997) 1 SCC 416 — Custodial Arrest Safeguards:** Laid down 11 mandatory guidelines governing arrest and detention—including visible officer identification, arrest memo attestation, informing relatives, and medical examination—subsequently codified into BNSS Sections 36, 47, 48, and 53.
* ***Lalita Kumari v. Govt. of Uttar Pradesh*** **(2014) 2 SCC 1 — Mandatory FIR Registration:** Held that registration of an FIR (now BNSS Sec. 173) is mandatory if the information discloses a cognizable offence, and no preliminary inquiry is permissible except in specific categories (matrimonial disputes, commercial offences, medical negligence, corruption, or abnormal delay).
* ***Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar*** **(2014) 8 SCC 273 — Anti-Mechanical Arrest Rule:** Directed police not to automatically arrest in offences punishable with imprisonment up to 7 years (including dowry cruelty under BNS Sec. 85) without satisfying the necessity checklist and issuing a Notice of Appearance (now BNSS Sec. 35(3)).
* ***Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi)*** **(2020) 5 SCC 1 — Anticipatory Bail Duration:** Constitution Bench held that anticipatory bail (now BNSS Sec. 482) should not invariably be limited to a fixed period and can continue until the end of the trial unless special circumstances warrant restriction.
* ***Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal*** **(2020) 7 SCC 1 — Electronic Evidence Certificate:** Clarified that the statutory certificate (now BSA Sec. 63) is a mandatory condition precedent for admitting secondary electronic evidence, excused only where the original device itself is produced as primary evidence.

# PART II: GENERAL CIVIL & COMMERCIAL LAW FRAMEWORK

## 1. THE CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE (CPC), 1908
*(Civil Adjudication — 158 Sections & 51 Orders)*

### 1.1 Foundational Doctrines of Civil Jurisdiction (Sections 9–25)
* **Jurisdiction of Civil Courts (Section 9):** Civil courts have jurisdiction to try all suits of a civil nature excepting suits of which their cognizance is either expressly barred (by a statute creating an exclusive tribunal) or impliedly barred (by public policy).
* **Res Sub Judice / Stay of Suit (Section 10):** No Court shall proceed with the trial of any suit in which the matter in issue is also directly and substantially in issue in a previously instituted suit between the same parties, litigating under the same title, pending in the same or any other competent Court in India. Prevents parallel trials and conflicting decrees.
* **Res Judicata (Section 11):** No Court shall try any suit or issue in which the matter directly and substantially in issue has been directly and substantially in issue in a former suit between the same parties, litigating under the same title, in a competent Court, and has been **heard and finally decided**.
  * *Constructive Res Judicata (Explanation IV):* Any matter which might and ought to have been made a ground of defence or attack in such former suit is deemed to have been directly and substantially in issue.
* **Territorial & Pecuniary Place of Suing (Sections 15–20):**
  * Every suit must be instituted in the Court of the lowest grade competent to try it (Sec. 15).
  * *Immovable Property (Sec. 16):* Suits for recovery, partition, foreclosure, redemption, or determination of rights in immovable property must be instituted in the Court within the local limits of whose jurisdiction the property is situated.
  * *Other Suits (Sec. 20):* Suits for money recovery, contract breach, or personal wrongs must be instituted where: (a) the defendant voluntarily resides, carries on business, or personally works for gain, OR (b) the **cause of action**, wholly or in part, arises.

### 1.2 Pleadings, Rejection of Plaint & Written Statement (Orders VI–VIII)
* **Amendment of Pleadings (Order VI Rule 17):** The Court may at any stage allow either party to alter or amend pleadings as necessary to determine the real questions in controversy. However, **no application for amendment shall be allowed after the trial has commenced**, unless the Court concludes that despite due diligence, the party could not have raised the matter before trial commenced.
* **Return vs. Rejection of Plaint (Order VII Rules 10–11):**
  * *Return (Rule 10):* Plaint is returned at any stage to be presented to the proper Court if the filing Court lacks territorial or pecuniary jurisdiction.
  * *Rejection (Rule 11):* A plaint **shall be rejected** at the threshold on 6 grounds: (a) where it does not disclose a cause of action, (b) where relief claimed is undervalued and not corrected, (c) where plaint is insufficiently stamped and not corrected, (d) where the suit appears from the statement in the plaint to be **barred by any law** (e.g., limitation), (e) where it is not filed in duplicate, or (f) where plaintiff fails to comply with Rule 9 summons formalities.
* **Written Statement, Set-Off & Counter-Claim (Order VIII Rules 1, 6, 6A):**
  * *Filing Timeline (Rule 1):* Defendant must file Written Statement within **30 days** from service of summons, extendable up to **90 days** for recorded reasons (in Commercial Courts, the **120-day** limit is mandatory and strictly non-extendable, forfeiting the right to file).
  * *Set-Off (Rule 6):* Defendant's claim for an ascertained sum of money legally recoverable from the plaintiff, acting as a shield to reduce/extinguish the plaintiff's money claim.
  * *Counter-Claim (Rule 6A):* A cross-suit by the defendant against the plaintiff for any right or claim (whether money or otherwise) accruing before the defendant delivers his defence; treated as a separate plaint even if the plaintiff's suit is stayed or dismissed.

### 1.3 Interlocutory Reliefs, Summary Suits & ADR (Sections 89, 148A, 151 & Orders XXXVII–XL)
* **Alternative Dispute Resolution — ADR (Section 89):** Where it appears to the Court that there exist elements of a settlement acceptable to the parties, the Court shall formulate the terms of settlement and refer them for: (a) Arbitration, (b) Conciliation, (c) Judicial settlement including Lok Adalat, or (d) Mediation.
* **Right to Lodge a Caveat (Section 148A):** Any person claiming a right to appear before the Court on the hearing of an expected application against them may lodge a caveat. Once lodged, the Court cannot pass an *ex parte* interim order without serving notice on the caveator. Remains in force for **90 days**.
* **Inherent Powers of Court (Section 151):** Nothing in the Code limits or affects the inherent power of the Court to make such orders as may be necessary for the ends of justice or to prevent abuse of the process of the Court.
* **Summary Suits (Order XXXVII):** Applicable to suits upon bills of exchange, hundies, promissory notes, and written contracts/guarantees for liquidated debts. The defendant has no automatic right to defend and must apply for **leave to defend within 10 days** of service of summons; if no triable issue is shown, the plaintiff gets an immediate decree.
* **Temporary Injunctions (Order XXXIX Rules 1–2A):** Granted to preserve property in dispute from waste/alienation or prevent breach of contract/injury pending suit. Plaintiff must satisfy the **Triple Test**: (1) *Prima facie* case, (2) Balance of convenience in plaintiff's favour, and (3) Irreparable injury that cannot be compensated by damages. Disobedience invites attachment of property or civil detention up to 3 months (Rule 2A).

### 1.4 Execution, Appeals, Review & Revision (Sections 36–115 & Order XXI)
* **Execution of Decrees (Sections 36–74 & Order XXI):**
  * Executed by the Court which passed the decree or the Court to which it is sent. All questions relating to execution, discharge, or satisfaction of the decree must be determined by the executing Court and not by a separate suit (Sec. 47).
  * *Modes (Sec. 51):* Delivery of property, attachment and sale of property, arrest and detention in civil prison (women are exempt from arrest in money decrees under Sec. 56), appointing a receiver.
* **First Appeal vs. Second Appeal (Sections 96–103):**
  * *First Appeal (Sec. 96):* Lies as a matter of statutory right from every original decree on **both questions of fact and questions of law**.
  * *Second Appeal (Sec. 100):* Lies to the High Court from an appellate decree **strictly if the High Court is satisfied that the case involves a substantial question of law**. Concurrent findings of fact cannot be disturbed.
* **Reference, Review & Revision (Sections 113–115):**
  * *Reference (Sec. 113):* Subordinate court seeks the opinion of the High Court on the validity of an Act/Ordinance/Regulation necessary for disposal of a case.
  * *Review (Sec. 114):* Re-examination of a judgment by the *same Court* on grounds of: (1) discovery of new and important matter/evidence not within knowledge despite due diligence, (2) mistake or error apparent on the face of the record, or (3) any other sufficient reason.
  * *Revision (Sec. 115):* High Court's supervisory power to correct **jurisdictional errors** (exercising jurisdiction not vested, failing to exercise vested jurisdiction, or acting illegally/with material irregularity) committed by a subordinate court in cases where no appeal lies.

## 2. THE INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872
*(Substantive Commercial & Contractual Law — 238 Sections)*

### 2.1 Formation & Essentials of a Valid Contract (Sections 2–30)
* **Offer & Acceptance (Sections 2–9):** A proposal (Sec. 2(a)) when accepted becomes a promise (Sec. 2(b)). Acceptance must be absolute, unqualified, and communicated in a usual/prescribed manner (Sec. 7). Under the Postal Rule (Sec. 4), communication of acceptance is complete against the proposer when put in a course of transmission so as to be out of the acceptor's power, and against the acceptor when it comes to the knowledge of the proposer.
* **Essentials of a Contract (Section 10):** All agreements are contracts if made by: (1) free consent of (2) parties competent to contract, (3) for a lawful consideration and (4) with a lawful object, and (5) are not expressly declared to be void.
* **Competence to Contract (Sections 11–12):** Every person is competent who is of the age of majority (18 years), of sound mind, and not disqualified from contracting by any law. An agreement with a minor is **void ab initio** (*Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*).
* **Free Consent & Vitiating Factors (Sections 13–22):** Consent is *not* free when caused by:
  1. **Coercion (Sec. 15):** Committing or threatening to commit any act forbidden by the BNS, or unlawful detaining/threatening to detain property, to the prejudice of any person, with intent to cause any person to enter into an agreement. *(Makes contract voidable).*
  2. **Undue Influence (Sec. 16):** Where relations subsisting between parties are such that one is in a position to dominate the will of the other (real/apparent authority, fiduciary relation, or mental distress) and uses that position to obtain an unfair advantage. *(Makes contract voidable).*
  3. **Fraud (Sec. 17):** Intentional deception via active suggestion of an untrue fact, active concealment of a fact, promise made without intention of performing it, or any other act fitted to deceive. *(Makes contract voidable + right to damages).*
  4. **Misrepresentation (Sec. 18):** Innocent/unwarranted positive assertion of an untrue fact believed to be true, or breach of duty without intent to deceive. *(Makes contract voidable, unless truth could be discovered with ordinary diligence).*
  5. **Mistake (Secs. 20–22):** Bilateral mistake of fact essential to the agreement renders the agreement **void** (Sec. 20); mistake of Indian law or unilateral mistake of fact does not render a contract voidable (Secs. 21–22).
* **Expressly Void Agreements (Sections 24–30):**
  * **Agreements without Consideration (Sec. 25):** Void, *except*: (1) written and registered agreement made out of natural love and affection between near relatives, (2) promise to compensate a person who has already voluntarily done something for the promisor, or (3) written and signed promise to pay a time-barred debt.
  * **Restraint of Marriage (Sec. 26):** Every agreement in restraint of the marriage of any person, other than a minor, is void.
  * **Restraint of Trade (Sec. 27):** Every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade, or business of any kind is to that extent void (sole statutory exception: sale of goodwill within reasonable local limits).
  * **Restraint of Legal Proceedings (Sec. 28):** Agreements restricting a party from enforcing contractual rights through ordinary legal tribunals, or curtailing the statutory limitation period, are void.
  * **Wagering Agreements (Sec. 30):** Agreements by way of wager (betting on an uncertain future event) are void and unenforceable.

### 2.2 Performance, Frustration & Quasi-Contracts (Sections 37–72)
* **Time as the Essence of Contract (Section 55):** When a party promises to do a thing at or before a specified time and fails to do so, the contract becomes voidable at the option of the promisee **if the intention of the parties was that time should be of the essence** (typical in commercial/mercantile contracts, generally presumed *not* to be of the essence in immovable property sales unless expressly stipulated).
* **Doctrine of Frustration / Supervening Impossibility (Section 56):** A contract to do an act which, after the contract is made, becomes impossible, or by reason of some event which the promisor could not prevent, unlawful, becomes **void** when the act becomes impossible or unlawful (e.g., destruction of subject matter, change of law, outbreak of war, death/incapacity in personal service contracts). Commercial hardship or price rise does *not* constitute frustration.
* **Novation, Rescission & Alteration (Section 62):** If the parties to a contract agree to substitute a new contract for it, or to rescind or alter it, the original contract need not be performed.
* **Quasi-Contracts (Sections 68–72):** Obligations imposed by law based on the principle of preventing **unjust enrichment** even without a formal agreement:
  * Claim for necessaries supplied to a person incapable of contracting (Sec. 68).
  * Reimbursement of a person paying money due by another in payment of which he is interested (Sec. 69).
  * Obligation of a person enjoying the benefit of a non-gratuitous act (Sec. 70).
  * Responsibility of a finder of goods (Sec. 71 — same duties as a bailee).
  * Liability of a person to whom money is paid or thing delivered by mistake or under coercion (Sec. 72).

### 2.3 Breach of Contract & Special Contracts (Sections 73–238)
* **Damages for Breach (Sections 73–74):**
  * *Unliquidated Damages (Sec. 73):* Party suffering breach is entitled to receive compensation for any loss or damage which naturally arose in the usual course of things from such breach, or which the parties knew when they made the contract to be likely to result from breach. Compensation is **not** given for any remote and indirect loss.
  * *Liquidated Damages / Penalty (Sec. 74):* Where a sum is named in the contract as payable upon breach, the complaining party is entitled (whether or not actual loss is proved) to receive **reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount so named**.
* **Indemnity & Guarantee (Sections 124–147):**
  * *Indemnity (Sec. 124):* A contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by the conduct of the promisor himself or any other person (Two parties; primary liability).
  * *Guarantee (Sec. 126):* A contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default (Three parties: Surety, Principal Debtor, Creditor; Surety's liability is co-extensive with that of the principal debtor under Sec. 128, and secondary).
* **Bailment & Pledge (Sections 148–181):**
  * *Bailment (Sec. 148):* Delivery of goods by one person to another for some purpose, upon a contract that they shall, when the purpose is accomplished, be returned or disposed of according to the directions of the person delivering them.
  * *Pledge (Sec. 172):* The bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt or performance of a promise.
* **Agency (Sections 182–238):** An "Agent" is employed to do any act for another ("Principal") or represent him in dealings with third persons. Contracts entered into through an agent have the exact same legal consequences as if entered into by the principal in person (Sec. 226 — *Qui facit per alium facit per se*).

## 3. SPECIFIC RELIEF, PROPERTY, REGISTRATION & LIMITATION LAWS

### 3.1 Specific Relief Act, 1963 (Amended 2018)
* **Suit for Dispossession (Section 6):** If any person is dispossessed of immovable property without his consent otherwise than in due course of law, he may recover possession by suit within **6 months** from dispossession, without proving title. No appeal or review lies from any order/decree passed under this section.
* **Mandatory Specific Performance (Section 10):** Following the 2018 Amendment, specific performance of a contract is **no longer a discretionary equitable relief**; the Court *shall* enforce specific performance subject strictly to statutory bars under Sections 11(2), 14, and 16.
* **Non-Enforceable Contracts (Section 14):** Specific performance cannot be enforced for: (a) a contract where a party has obtained substituted performance under Sec. 20, (b) a contract involving performance of a continuous duty which the Court cannot supervise, (c) a contract dependent on the personal qualifications of the parties, and (d) a contract which is in its nature determinable.
* **Substituted Performance (Section 20):** Where a contract is broken due to non-performance by one party, the aggrieved party has the option of substituted performance through a third party or own agency, and recovering the expenses and other costs actually incurred from the defaulting party, **after giving at least 30 days' prior written notice**.
* **Bar on Injunctions in Infrastructure Projects (Sections 20A & 41(ha)):** No injunction shall be granted by a Court in a suit involving a contract relating to an infrastructure project (transport, energy, water, communication, social/commercial infrastructure) where granting an injunction would cause impediment or delay in its progress or completion.
* **Declaratory Decrees (Section 34):** Any person entitled to any legal character, or to any right as to any property, may institute a suit against any person denying or interested to deny his title to such character or right.

### 3.2 Transfer of Property Act, 1882 & Registration Act, 1908
* **Non-Transferable Properties (Section 6 TPA):** Includes the chance of an heir-apparent succeeding to an estate (*spes successionis*), a mere right of re-entry for breach of a condition subsequent, an easement apart from the dominant heritage, a right to future maintenance, a mere right to sue, and a public office or stipend.
* **Rule Against Perpetuity (Section 14 TPA):** No transfer of property can operate to create an interest which is to take effect after the lifetime of one or more persons living at the date of such transfer, and the minority of some person who shall be in existence at the expiration of that period, and to whom, if he attains full age, the interest created is to belong.
* **Doctrine of Lis Pendens (Section 52 TPA):** During the pendency in any competent Court of any suit or proceeding (not collusive) in which any right to immovable property is directly and specifically in question, the property **cannot be transferred or otherwise dealt with** by any party to the suit so as to affect the rights of any other party thereto under any decree or order which may be made therein, except under the authority of the Court.
* **Doctrine of Part Performance (Section 53A TPA):** Protects a transferee who has taken possession of immovable property in part performance of a **written and registered contract** for consideration and has performed or is willing to perform his part of the contract, barring the transferor from enforcing any right against the transferee other than a right expressly provided by the contract.
* **Compulsory Registration & Effect of Non-Registration (Sections 17 & 49 Registration Act):**
  * Instruments of gift of immovable property, non-testamentary instruments creating/extinguishing rights in immovable property valued at **₹100 or upwards**, leases exceeding 1 year, and agreements to sell under Sec. 53A TPA **must be registered** within 4 months of execution (Sec. 17 & 23).
  * Under Section 49, an unregistered document required to be registered **shall not affect any immovable property** comprised therein, nor be received as evidence of any transaction affecting such property (admissible only for collateral purposes).

### 3.3 The Limitation Act, 1963
* **Bar of Limitation (Section 3):** Every suit instituted, appeal preferred, and application made after the prescribed period **shall be dismissed**, although limitation has not been set up as a defence.
* **Condonation of Delay (Section 5):** Applies **only to appeals and applications** (never to original suits or execution applications). The Court may admit an appeal/application after the prescribed period if the appellant/applicant satisfies the Court that they had **sufficient cause** for not preferring it within time.
* **Acknowledgment in Writing (Section 18):** Where, before the expiration of the prescribed period for a suit/application, an acknowledgment of liability is made in writing and signed by the party against whom such property or right is claimed, a **fresh period of limitation** shall be computed from the time when the acknowledgment was so signed.

## 4. NEGOTIABLE INSTRUMENTS ACT, 1881 — CHEQUE DISHONOUR (SECTIONS 138–148)
*(Quasi-Criminal Commercial Liability for Dishonour of Cheques)*

### 4.1 Ingredients & Statutory Timelines (Sections 138 & 142)
* **Offence of Dishonour of Cheque (Section 138):** Where any cheque drawn by a person on an account maintained by him with a banker for payment of any amount of money to another person from out of that account for the discharge, in whole or in part, of any **legally enforceable debt or other liability**, is returned by the bank unpaid (either because of insufficiency of funds or because it exceeds the arrangement made with the bank), such person shall be deemed to have committed an offence.
  * *Punishment:* Imprisonment for a term up to **2 years**, or with fine which may extend to **twice the amount of the cheque**, or with both.
* **Three Mandatory Provisos & Strict Timeline Chain (Section 138 Provisos & Section 142):**
  1. **Presentation (Proviso (a)):** Cheque must be presented to the bank within **3 months** from the date on which it is drawn (per RBI guidelines) or within its period of validity, whichever is earlier.
  2. **Statutory Demand Notice (Proviso (b)):** Payee/holder in due course must make a demand for payment of the cheque amount by giving a notice in writing to the drawer within **30 days** of the receipt of information from the bank regarding the return of the cheque as unpaid.
  3. **Grace Period to Pay (Proviso (c)):** Drawer fails to make payment of the said amount to the payee within **15 days** of the receipt of the said notice. Cause of action arises on the **16th day**.
  4. **Filing of Complaint (Sec. 142(1)(b)):** Written complaint must be filed before a Judicial Magistrate of First Class / Metropolitan Magistrate within **1 month** from the date on which the cause of action arises (delay condonable upon showing sufficient cause).
* **Territorial Jurisdiction (Section 142(2)):**
  * If the cheque is delivered for collection through an account: Jurisdiction lies exclusively with the Court within whose local jurisdiction the **branch of the bank where the payee maintains the account** is situated.
  * If the cheque is presented for payment over the counter (otherwise than through an account): Jurisdiction lies with the Court where the **branch of the drawee bank (drawer's bank)** is situated.

### 4.2 Statutory Presumptions, Corporate Liability & Interim Compensation (Sections 139–148)
* **Presumption in Favour of Holder (Section 139):** It shall be presumed, unless the contrary is proved, that the holder of a cheque received the cheque for the discharge, in whole or in part, of any debt or other liability (*Rebuttable presumption of law*).
* **Defence Not Allowed (Section 140):** It is not a defence in a prosecution under Sec. 138 that the drawer had no reason to believe when he issued the cheque that the cheque may be dishonoured on presentment.
* **Offences by Companies (Section 141):** If the person committing an offence under Sec. 138 is a company (including a partnership firm/LLP), every person who, at the time the offence was committed, was **in charge of, and was responsible to the company for the conduct of the business of the company**, as well as the company, shall be deemed to be guilty of the offence (excluding independent/non-executive directors not involved in day-to-day management).
* **Summary Trial & Compounding (Sections 143 & 147):** All offences under Chapter XVII are tried summarily (Sec. 143) with endeavour to conclude trial within **6 months**, and every offence punishable under the Act is **compoundable** (Sec. 147 — can be settled by compromise at any stage).
* **Interim Compensation & Appellate Deposit (Sections 143A & 148):**
  * *Interim Compensation at Trial (Sec. 143A):* Trial Court may order the drawer to pay interim compensation to the complainant (where drawer pleads not guilty) not exceeding **20% of the amount of the cheque**, payable within **60 days** (extendable by 30 days).
  * *Deposit Pending Appeal (Sec. 148):* Appellate Court may order the convicted appellant to deposit a minimum of **20% of the fine or compensation** awarded by the trial Court (in addition to interim compensation paid under Sec. 143A).

## 5. ARBITRATION AND CONCILIATION ACT, 1996
*(Alternative Dispute Resolution & Enforcement of Arbitral Awards)*

### 5.1 Arbitration Agreement, Court Referral & Interim Relief (Sections 7–17)
* **Arbitration Agreement (Section 7):** An agreement by the parties to submit to arbitration all or certain disputes which have arisen or which may arise between them in respect of a defined legal relationship (contractual or not). Must be **in writing** (may be in the form of an arbitration clause in a contract, a separate agreement, exchange of letters/emails/electronic communication, or exchange of statements of claim and defence).
* **Mandatory Judicial Referral to Arbitration (Section 8):** A judicial authority before which an action is brought in a matter which is the subject of an arbitration agreement **shall**, if a party so applies not later than the date of submitting his first statement on the substance of the dispute, refer the parties to arbitration unless it finds that *prima facie* no valid arbitration agreement exists.
* **Interim Measures by Court vs. Tribunal (Sections 9 & 17):**
  * *By Court (Sec. 9):* A party may apply to a Court before, during, or after arbitral proceedings (before enforcement) for interim custody/preservation of goods, securing the amount in dispute, interim injunction, or appointment of a receiver. Once constituted, the Court will not entertain a Sec. 9 application unless remedies under Sec. 17 are inefficacious. If interim relief is granted before commencement, arbitral proceedings must commence within **90 days**.
  * *By Arbitral Tribunal (Sec. 17):* Arbitral tribunal possesses the exact same powers to grant interim measures during proceedings as a Civil Court, and its interim orders are enforceable like an order of the Court.

### 5.2 Appointment, Independence & Jurisdiction of Arbitrators (Sections 11–16)
* **Appointment of Arbitrators (Section 11):** Parties are free to agree on a procedure (sole arbitrator or odd number of arbitrators). Failing agreement within 30 days, appointment is made upon request by the **Supreme Court** (in International Commercial Arbitration) or the **High Court** (in Domestic Arbitration), confining examination strictly to the *existence* of an arbitration agreement (Sec. 11(6A)).
* **Independence, Impartiality & Ineligibility (Section 12 & Schedules V/VII):**
  * Prospective arbitrator must disclose in writing any circumstances likely to give rise to justifiable doubts as to independence or impartiality (Fifth Schedule grounds).
  * *Ineligibility (Sec. 12(5) & Seventh Schedule):* Any person whose relationship with the parties, counsel, or subject matter falls under the Seventh Schedule (e.g., employee, consultant, advisor, or unilaterally appointed interested party) is **statutorily ineligible** to be appointed as an arbitrator unless waived by an express written agreement *after* disputes have arisen.
* **Kompetenz-Kompetenz Principle (Section 16):** The arbitral tribunal is competent to rule on its own jurisdiction, including ruling on any objections with respect to the existence or validity of the arbitration agreement. An arbitration clause is treated as an independent agreement separable from the rest of the contract.

### 5.3 Arbitral Procedure, Timelines & Challenging/Enforcing Awards (Sections 23–36)
* **Fast-Track Procedure (Section 29B):** Parties may agree in writing to have their dispute resolved by a fast-track procedure (decided on written pleadings/documents without oral hearing), with the award required within **6 months**.
* **Time Limit for Arbitral Award (Section 29A):**
  * Pleadings (Statement of Claim and Defence) must be completed within **6 months** from appointment of arbitrators (Sec. 23(4)).
  * Award in domestic arbitrations must be made within **12 months** from the date of completion of pleadings (extendable by mutual consent of parties for a further **6 months**; any further extension requires Court permission).
* **Recourse Against Arbitral Award — Setting Aside (Section 34):** An arbitral award may be set aside by the Court **only** if the party proves:
  * Incapacity of a party or invalidity of the arbitration agreement;
  * Lack of proper notice of appointment/proceedings or inability to present case;
  * Award deals with a dispute not contemplated by or falling outside the terms of submission to arbitration;
  * Composition of tribunal or arbitral procedure was not in accordance with party agreement;
  * Subject matter of dispute is not capable of settlement by arbitration under law;
  * Award is in **conflict with the Public Policy of India** (induced by fraud/corruption, in contravention with fundamental policy of Indian law, or in conflict with most basic notions of morality/justice); OR
  * In domestic arbitrations, the award is vitiated by **patent illegality appearing on the face of the award** (Sec. 34(2A) — Court cannot re-appreciate evidence).
  * *Limitation:* Application under Sec. 34 must be filed within **3 months** from receipt of the award (extendable by a maximum of **30 days** on sufficient cause, and not thereafter).
* **Finality & Enforcement of Award (Sections 35–36):** An arbitral award is final and binding on the parties (Sec. 35). After the time for making an application to set aside under Sec. 34 has expired, the award shall be enforced in accordance with the provisions of the **Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, in the same manner as if it were a decree of the Court** (Sec. 36). Filing a Sec. 34 challenge does **not** automatically stay enforcement unless a separate stay order is granted by the Court (subject to deposit conditions).

## 6. LANDMARK SUPREME COURT PRECEDENTS IN CIVIL & COMMERCIAL LAW
* ***Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*** **(1903) ILR 30 Cal 539 — Minor's Contract Void Ab Initio:** Established that under Section 11 of the Indian Contract Act, a minor is wholly incompetent to contract, and any agreement entered into by a minor is void from the very beginning (*void ab initio*), not merely voidable.
* ***Satyabrata Ghose v. Mugneeram Bangur & Co.*** **(1954) SCR 310 — Doctrine of Frustration:** Clarified that "impossibility" under Section 56 of the Contract Act is not restricted to literal physical impossibility, but encompasses any supervening event that destroys the fundamental foundation and purpose of the contract.
* ***Salem Advocate Bar Association v. Union of India*** **(2005) 6 SCC 344 — CPC Amendments & Section 89 ADR:** Upheld the constitutionality of the CPC Amendments (1999/2002) and framed model rules for court-annexed mediation, conciliation, and Lok Adalat referrals under Section 89 CPC.
* ***Kailash Nanhku v. State of U.P.*** **(2005) 4 SCC 480 — Written Statement Timeline:** Held that the 90-day limit under Order VIII Rule 1 CPC for filing a Written Statement in non-commercial civil suits is directory (extendable in exceptional circumstances), whereas in Commercial Suits (under the Commercial Courts Act, 2015), the 120-day limit is strictly mandatory (*SCG Contracts India Pvt. Ltd. v. K.S. Chamankar Infrastructure*).
* ***Dashrath Rupsingh Rathod v. State of Maharashtra*** **(2014) 9 SCC 129 & 2015 Amendment (Sec. 142(2) NI Act):** Following the Supreme Court's ruling on cheque dishonour jurisdiction, Parliament enacted Section 142(2) to confer exclusive jurisdiction on the Court where the **payee's bank branch** (account for collection) is located.
* ***Perkins Eastman Architects DPC v. HSCC (India) Ltd.*** **(2020) 20 SCC 760 — Unilateral Arbitrator Appointment Bar:** Held that a party having an interest in the outcome of the dispute is ineligible to unilaterally appoint a sole arbitrator under Section 12(5) read with the Seventh Schedule of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.

# PART III: CONSUMER PROTECTION & REAL ESTATE FRAMEWORK

## 1. THE CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT, 2019
*(Consumer Rights, Regulatory Enforcement & Quasi-Judicial Redressal — 107 Sections)*

### 1.1 Core Definitions & Six Statutory Consumer Rights (Section 2)
* **Who is a "Consumer"? (Section 2(7)):**
  * Includes any person who buys any goods or hires/avails any services for a consideration which has been paid, promised, partly paid and partly promised, or under any system of deferred payment.
  * Explicitly covers both **offline and online transactions** through electronic means, teleshopping, direct selling, or multi-level marketing.
  * *Commercial Purpose Exclusion:* Excludes a person who obtains goods for resale or for any commercial purpose. However, the Explanation safeguards small entrepreneurs by clarifying that "commercial purpose" **does not include** use by a person of goods bought and used by him exclusively for the purpose of earning his livelihood, by means of self-employment.
* **Six Statutory Consumer Rights (Section 2(9)):**
  1. **Right to Safety:** Protection against marketing of goods, products, or services hazardous to life and property.
  2. **Right to be Informed:** Information about quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard, and price of goods, products, or services to protect against unfair trade practices.
  3. **Right to Choose:** Assurance of access, wherever possible, to a variety of goods, products, or services at competitive prices.
  4. **Right to be Heard:** Assurance that consumer interests will receive due consideration at appropriate fora.
  5. **Right to Seek Redressal:** Right to seek redressal against unfair trade practices, restrictive trade practices, or unscrupulous exploitation.
  6. **Right to Consumer Awareness:** Right to acquire knowledge and skills to be an informed consumer.
* **Defect vs. Deficiency (Sections 2(10) & 2(11)):**
  * *Defect (Goods):* Any fault, imperfection, or shortcoming in quality, quantity, potency, purity, or standard required to be maintained under law or contract.
  * *Deficiency (Services):* Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in quality, nature, and manner of performance required under law or contract; explicitly includes any act of negligence, omission, or commission causing loss/injury, and **deliberate withholding of relevant information** by the service provider.
* **Unfair Contracts (Section 2(46)):** Contracts between a manufacturer/trader/service provider and a consumer containing terms that cause a significant change in consumer rights to their detriment, specifically targeting 6 abusive clauses:
  1. Requiring manifestly excessive security deposits from a consumer.
  2. Imposing any penalty on the consumer for breach of contract which is wholly disproportionate to the loss occurred.
  3. Refusing to accept early repayment of debts on payment of applicable penalty.
  4. Entitling a party to terminate such contract unilaterally without reasonable cause.
  5. Permitting or having the effect of permitting one party to assign the contract to the detriment of the consumer without his consent.
  6. Imposing on the consumer any unreasonable charge, obligation, or condition which puts such consumer to disadvantage.
* **Unfair Trade Practices (Section 2(47)):** Encompasses deceptive practices to promote sale/use/supply of goods or services, including false quality claims, misleading bargain prices, fake contests, hoarding, selling spurious goods, and three modern consumer protections:
  1. Failing to issue a bill, cash memo, or receipt for goods sold or services rendered.
  2. Refusing after selling goods or rendering services to take back or withdraw defective goods or deficient services and refund the consideration within the stipulated period (or within **30 days** if no period is stipulated).
  3. Disclosing to any other person any **personal information** given in confidence by the consumer (unless required by law or in public interest).

### 1.2 Central Consumer Protection Authority — CCPA (Sections 10–27)
* **Mandate & Executive Powers (Sections 10, 18, 20):** The CCPA is a statutory regulatory authority empowered to protect, promote, and enforce the rights of consumers **as a class** (preventing class-wide injury). It can conduct investigations through its Director-General (Sec. 15), file complaints before Consumer Commissions, intervene in proceedings, order the **recall of dangerous/hazardous goods**, order reimbursement of prices of recalled goods, and cancel licenses (Sec. 20).
* **Penalties Against Misleading Advertisements & Endorsers (Section 21):**
  * CCPA may order discontinuation or modification of a false or misleading advertisement and impose a penalty of up to **₹10 Lakhs** on the manufacturer or endorser (rising up to **₹50 Lakhs** for every subsequent contravention).
  * CCPA may prohibit the **celebrity/influencer endorser** of a false or misleading advertisement from making any endorsement of any product or service for up to **1 year** (and up to **3 years** for subsequent contraventions).
  * *Due Diligence Defence (Sec. 21(5)):* No endorser is liable to penalty if they exercised due diligence to verify the veracity of the claims made in the advertisement regarding the product or service endorsed.

### 1.3 Three-Tier Quasi-Judicial Commissions & Procedure (Sections 28–73)
* **Pecuniary Jurisdiction (Based on Value of Consideration Actually Paid):**
  1. **District Commission (Sec. 34):** Up to **₹50 Lakhs**.
  2. **State Commission (Sec. 47):** Above **₹50 Lakhs up to ₹2 Crore** (also hears complaints against **Unfair Contracts** where value paid is up to ₹10 Crore).
  3. **National Commission — NCDRC (Sec. 58):** Above **₹2 Crore** (also hears complaints against **Unfair Contracts** where value paid exceeds ₹10 Crore).
* **Consumer-Friendly Territorial Jurisdiction (Section 34(2)):** A complaint can be instituted in the District Commission within whose local limits: (a) the opposite party resides or carries on business or has a branch office, (b) the cause of action wholly or in part arises, OR **(c) the complainant resides or personally works for gain**.
* **Procedural Timelines & Appeals (Sections 36, 38, 41, 51, 67, 69):**
  * *Limitation Period (Sec. 69):* **2 Years** from the date on which the cause of action arises (condonable upon showing sufficient cause).
  * *Admissibility (Sec. 36):* Must be decided within **21 days** of filing; if not decided within 21 days, the complaint is **deemed to have been admitted**.
  * *Disposal Timeline (Sec. 38):* Written version to be filed within 30 days (extendable by max 15 days). Commissions must endeavour to decide complaints within **3 months** (or **5 months** if laboratory testing/analysis is required).
  * *Appeals & Mandatory Pre-Deposit:* Appeal from District to State Commission within **45 days** (Sec. 41); State to National Commission within **30 days** on a substantial question of law (Sec. 51); National Commission to Supreme Court within **30 days** (Sec. 67). No appeal shall be entertained unless the appellant deposits **50% of the amount** ordered by the lower Commission.
* **Enforcement & Penalties for Non-Compliance (Sections 71–72):** Every order of a Consumer Commission is enforced in the exact same manner as a decree of a Civil Court (Sec. 71). Failure to comply with any order is a criminal offence punishable by the Commission (exercising powers of a Judicial Magistrate of First Class) with imprisonment from **1 month to 3 years**, or fine from **₹25,000 to ₹1 Lakh**, or both (Sec. 72).

### 1.4 Strict Product Liability & Criminal Offences (Sections 82–93)
* **Product Liability Action (Sections 82–87):** Establishes statutory strict liability allowing a complainant to claim compensation for any harm (property damage, personal injury, illness, death, or mental agony) caused by a defective product:
  * *Product Manufacturer Liability (Sec. 84):* Liable if the product contains a manufacturing defect, is defective in design, deviates from manufacturing specifications, fails to conform to an express warranty, or lacks adequate instructions/warnings regarding improper usage—**even if the manufacturer proves they were not negligent or fraudulent** in making the express warranty.
  * *Product Service Provider Liability (Sec. 85):* Liable if the service provided was faulty, imperfect, deficient, or inadequate, or failed to issue adequate warnings/instructions.
  * *Product Seller Liability (Sec. 86):* A seller (who is not the manufacturer) is liable if they exercised substantial control over designing/testing/packaging/labelling, altered/modified the product causing harm, made an independent express warranty, sold a product whose manufacturer is unknown/untraceable/unservable, or failed to exercise reasonable care in assembling, inspecting, or maintaining the product.
* **Criminal Offences & Penalties (Sections 88–91):**
  * *False/Misleading Advertisement (Sec. 89):* Manufacturer or service provider is punishable with imprisonment up to **2 years** and fine up to **₹10 Lakhs** (subsequent offence: up to **5 years** and fine up to **₹50 Lakhs**).
  * *Adulterated Products & Spurious Goods (Secs. 90–91):* Graded criminal punishment based on harm caused:
    * Causing no injury: Imprisonment up to 6 months + fine up to ₹1 Lakh (applies to adulterants).
    * Causing injury (not grievous hurt): Imprisonment up to 1 year + fine up to ₹3 Lakhs.
    * Causing grievous hurt: Imprisonment up to 7 years + fine up to ₹5 Lakhs.
    * Resulting in death: Imprisonment **not less than 7 years (extending to life)** + fine not less than ₹10 Lakhs.

## 2. E-COMMERCE RULES, 2020 & CCPA GUIDELINES

### 2.1 Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020
* **Broad Applicability (Rule 2):** Applies to all goods and services bought or sold over digital or electronic networks (covering both **Inventory** and **Marketplace** models), including foreign e-commerce entities not established in India that systematically offer goods or services to consumers in India.
* **Mandatory Duties of E-Commerce Entities (Rule 4):**
  * Appoint a **Nodal Officer** or alternate senior designated functionary **resident in India** to ensure compliance with the Act and Rules.
  * Prominently display legal name, principal geographic address, website details, and contact details (email, landline, mobile) of customer care and the **Grievance Officer**.
  * Acknowledge any consumer complaint within **48 hours** and redress the complaint within **1 month** from the date of receipt.
  * **Ban on Unilateral Cancellation Charges:** No e-commerce entity shall impose cancellation charges on consumers cancelling after confirming purchase unless similar charges are also borne by the e-commerce entity if they cancel the purchase order unilaterally.
  * **Explicit Consent Only:** Record consumer consent for purchase only through an **explicit and affirmative action**, and never automatically (banning pre-ticked checkboxes).
  * **No Price Manipulation:** Must not manipulate the price of goods or services offered on its platform in such a manner as to gain unreasonable profit by imposing unjustified prices, nor discriminate between consumers of the same class.
* **Transparency Duties of Marketplace Entities & Sellers (Rules 5 & 6):**
  * Display complete seller details (legal name, geographic address, customer care number, rating/aggregated feedback) and execute a prior written contract with sellers.
  * Display the **total price in a single figure** along with the breakup price showing all compulsory and voluntary charges (delivery, postage, handling, conveyance, and applicable taxes).
  * Mandatorily mention the **Country of Origin** of goods to enable informed consumer choice.
  * Sellers cannot falsely represent themselves as consumers, post fake reviews, or refuse to take back defective/spurious goods or refund consideration if delivery is late or deficient.

### 2.2 CCPA Guidelines on Dark Patterns (2023) & Service Charges (2022)
* **Prohibition of 13 Dark Patterns (2023 Guidelines):** Legally bans deceptive UI/UX design practices across all platforms, advertisers, and sellers that subvert consumer autonomy:
  1. *False Urgency:* Falsely stating or implying limited stock/time (e.g., fake countdown timers).
  2. *Basket Sneaking:* Automatically adding additional items, charity donations, or insurance to the checkout cart without explicit consent.
  3. *Confirm Shaming:* Using guilt, shame, or ridicule to nudge a user into opting in (e.g., *"No, I prefer paying full price"*).
  4. *Forced Action:* Forcing a user to buy an unrelated good, subscribe, or share personal contacts to complete an intended action.
  5. *Subscription Trap:* Making signing up easy while making cancellation complex, hidden, or requiring different modes.
  6. *Interface Interference:* Highlighting preferred options while obscuring/graying out cancellation or opt-out buttons.
  7. *Bait and Switch:* Advertising one quality/price outcome and silently substituting an inferior/costlier one at checkout.
  8. *Drip Pricing:* Concealing mandatory fees (convenience fees, platform fees, taxes) until the final payment screen.
  9. *Disguised Advertisement:* Masking paid advertisements as organic user reviews, editorial content, or search results.
  10. *Nagging:* Persistent, repetitive pop-ups disrupting the user to force an action unrelated to their transaction.
  11. *Trick Question:* Using confusing double negatives or deliberately ambiguous wording in opt-in/opt-out prompts.
  12. *SaaS Billing:* Quietly charging recurring subscription fees without prior notification or converting free trials into paid plans silently.
  13. *Rogue Malwares:* Using fake virus/security alerts to trick consumers into purchasing fake software or paying ransom.
* **Prohibition of Mandatory Service Charge in Hotels & Restaurants (2022 Guidelines):**
  * No hotel or restaurant shall add a "service charge" automatically or by default in the food bill, nor collect it under any other name.
  * Service charge is strictly **voluntary, optional, and at the consumer's discretion**. No hotel or restaurant can restrict entry or provision of services based on a consumer's refusal to pay a service charge, nor add it to the bill and levy GST on the total amount.

## 3. REAL ESTATE (REGULATION AND DEVELOPMENT) ACT (RERA), 2016
*(Homebuyer Protection & Real Estate Regulatory Compliance)*

### 3.1 Compulsory Registration & Promoter Obligations (Sections 3–17)
* **Compulsory Project Registration (Section 3):** No promoter shall advertise, market, book, sell, or offer for sale any plot, apartment, or building in any real estate project (where land area exceeds **500 sq. meters** or apartments exceed **8 units**) without registering the project with the State RERA Authority.
* **70% Escrow Account Mandate (Section 4(2)(l)(D)):** Promoter must deposit **70% of the amounts realized** from allottees into a separate scheduled bank account to cover the cost of construction and land cost, withdrawable only in proportion to the percentage of completion as certified by an engineer, architect, and chartered accountant.
* **Cap on Advance Payment (Section 13):** A promoter shall not accept a sum of **more than 10% of the cost** of the apartment/plot/building as an advance payment or application fee without first entering into a written, registered **Agreement for Sale**.
* **Structural Defect Liability (Section 14(3)):** If any structural defect or defect in workmanship/quality/provision of services is brought to the promoter's notice within **5 years** from the date of handing over possession, the promoter must rectify such defects without further charge within **30 days** (failing which the allottee is entitled to appropriate compensation).

### 3.2 Homebuyer Remedies, Refund with Interest & Adjudication (Sections 18–71)
* **Return of Amount & Compensation for Delay (Section 18):** If the promoter fails to complete or give possession of an apartment/plot in accordance with the terms of the Agreement for Sale:
  * *If allottee wishes to withdraw:* Promoter is obligated on demand to return the **entire amount received** with prescribed interest (typically SBI MCLR + 2%) and compensation.
  * *If allottee does not withdraw:* Promoter must pay **interest for every month of delay** till handing over of possession.
* **Concurrent Remedy with Consumer Commissions:** As held by the Supreme Court in *Imperia Structures Ltd. v. Anil Patni* (2020), remedies under RERA and the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 are **concurrent**, allowing an allottee to choose either forum (though not both simultaneously for the same relief).
* **Appeals (Sections 44 & 58):** Appeal from RERA Authority/Adjudicating Officer lies to the **Real Estate Appellate Tribunal (REAT)** within **60 days** (requires promoter to pre-deposit at least 30% to 100% of penalty/awarded amount under Sec. 43(5)), and further to the High Court within **60 days**.

## 4. LANDMARK SUPREME COURT PRECEDENTS IN CONSUMER & REAL ESTATE LAW
* ***Lucknow Development Authority v. M.K. Gupta*** **(1994) 1 SCC 243 — Public Authorities & Compensation for Mental Agony:** Held that statutory and public housing authorities performing services for consideration fall squarely within the definition of "service" under the Consumer Protection Act, and Consumer Commissions can award exemplary compensation for harassment and mental agony.
* ***Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha*** **(1995) 6 SCC 651 — Medical Negligence Under Consumer Jurisdiction:** Ruled that medical services rendered against payment of fees (whether paid directly by the patient or through insurance/employer) constitute "service" under the Consumer Protection Act (excluding only hospitals providing entirely free treatment to all patients).
* ***Laxmi Engineering Works v. P.S.G. Industrial Institute*** **(1995) 3 SCC 583 — Self-Employment Livelihood Exception:** Clarified that a person purchasing machinery or goods to operate personally for earning a livelihood by means of self-employment is a protected "consumer" and is not barred by the "commercial purpose" exclusion.
* ***Pioneer Urban Land & Infrastructure Ltd. v. Govindan Raghavan*** **(2019) 5 SCC 725 — One-Sided Builder Agreements as Unfair Trade Practice:** Held that incorporating wholly one-sided, oppressive clauses in flat-buyer agreements (now codified as "Unfair Contracts" under Sec. 2(46) CPA, 2019) constitutes an unfair trade practice, entitling homebuyers to full refund with interest rather than being forced to accept delayed possession.

# PART IV: CONSTITUTIONAL & WRIT JURISDICTION

## 1. FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS & REMEDIES (ARTICLES 12–35 & 226)
*(Direct Enforcement of Fundamental & Legal Rights Before High Courts and the Supreme Court)*

### 1.1 Core Fundamental Rights Invoked in Litigation
* **Definition of "State" (Article 12):** Fundamental Rights are enforceable against the Government and Parliament of India, Government and Legislature of each State, and all local or other authorities (municipalities, PSUs, statutory bodies, and instrumentalities of the State).
* **Right to Equality (Articles 14–18):** Guarantees equality before law and equal protection of laws (Art. 14 — strikes down arbitrary, unreasonable, or discriminatory State action), prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth (Art. 15), guarantees equality of opportunity in public employment (Art. 16), and abolishes untouchability (Art. 17).
* **Six Fundamental Freedoms (Article 19(1)):** Guarantees citizens freedom of: (a) speech and expression, (b) peaceful assembly without arms, (c) forming associations, unions, or cooperative societies, (d) free movement throughout India, (e) residence/settlement anywhere in India, and (g) practising any profession or carrying on any occupation, trade, or business (subject to reasonable restrictions under Arts. 19(2)–(6)).
* **Protection of Life and Personal Liberty (Article 21):** No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law (which must be fair, just, and reasonable). Judicially expanded to include the **Right to Privacy**, **Right to Livelihood**, **Right to Speedy Trial**, **Right to Clean Environment**, **Right to Health**, and **Right to Shelter**.
* **Protection Against Arrest and Detention (Article 22):** Right to be informed of grounds of arrest, right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of choice, mandatory production before a Magistrate within 24 hours, and strict safeguards against preventive detention.

### 1.2 Supreme Court (Article 32) vs. High Court (Article 226) Writ Jurisdiction
* **Article 32 (Supreme Court):** The right to move the Supreme Court by appropriate proceedings for the enforcement of **Fundamental Rights** is itself a guaranteed Fundamental Right ("Heart and Soul of the Constitution"). Cannot be invoked for non-fundamental statutory or contractual rights.
* **Article 226 (High Courts):** Confers **wider jurisdiction** than Article 32—High Courts can issue writs not only for the enforcement of **Fundamental Rights**, but also **"for any other purpose"** (enforcement of ordinary statutory/legal rights, challenging arbitrary administrative decisions, or correcting tribunal errors within their territorial jurisdiction).

## 2. THE FIVE CONSTITUTIONAL WRITS & PUBLIC INTEREST LITIGATION (PIL)

### 2.1 The Five Prerogative Writs
1. **Habeas Corpus ("You may have the body"):** Issued to produce a person who has been illegally detained (whether by police, State authorities, or private individuals in cases of unlawful confinement/child custody) before the Court to examine the legality of detention and order immediate release if unlawful. *Locus standi* is relaxed—any friend or relative can file.
2. **Mandamus ("We command"):** A command issued by a Constitutional Court directing a public official, government body, corporation, or inferior tribunal to perform a **public or statutory duty** which they have failed or refused to perform despite a legal demand. Cannot be issued against a private individual or to enforce a purely private contract.
3. **Certiorari ("To be certified / quashed"):** Issued by a superior court to an inferior court, tribunal, or quasi-judicial authority to transmit the record of proceedings and **quash an order already passed** on grounds of lack of jurisdiction, excess of jurisdiction, violation of principles of natural justice (*Audi Alteram Partem*), or an error of law apparent on the face of the record.
4. **Prohibition ("To forbid"):** Issued by a superior court to an inferior court or tribunal **while proceedings are still pending**, preventing it from continuing proceedings in excess of its jurisdiction or contrary to natural justice (*Prevention is better than cure*, whereas *Certiorari* quashes a completed order).
5. **Quo Warranto ("By what authority"):** Issued to inquire into the legality of a claim by a person to a **substantive public office**, ousting a usurper who lacks statutory qualifications or was appointed in violation of mandatory legal rules. Any citizen can file without showing personal injury.

### 2.2 Public Interest Litigation (PIL) & Curative Remedies
* **Public Interest Litigation (PIL):** Relaxes the traditional rule of *locus standi*, allowing any public-spirited citizen or organization acting *bona fide* to petition the High Court (Art. 226) or Supreme Court (Art. 32) on behalf of marginalized, impoverished, or voiceless sections of society whose Fundamental Rights are violated.
* **Curative Petition (Supreme Court):** Narrowest extraordinary constitutional remedy available *after* dismissal of a Review Petition under Article 137, permitted solely to prevent gross abuse of process or violation of principles of natural justice (*Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra*).

## 3. LANDMARK CONSTITUTIONAL PRECEDENTS
* ***Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala*** **(1973) 4 SCC 225 — Basic Structure Doctrine:** Held by a 13-Judge Bench that while Parliament has wide power to amend the Constitution under Article 368, it cannot alter or destroy the **Basic Structure** of the Constitution (supremacy of the Constitution, rule of law, judicial review, federalism, secularism, and separation of powers).
* ***Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India*** **(1978) 1 SCC 248 — Golden Triangle (Articles 14, 19 & 21):** Held that any "procedure established by law" under Article 21 must be right, just, and fair—not arbitrary, fanciful, or oppressive—interlinking Articles 14, 19, and 21.
* ***Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India*** **(2017) 10 SCC 1 — Right to Privacy:** A 9-Judge Bench unanimously declared that the **Right to Privacy** (including informational privacy and bodily autonomy) is an intrinsic part of the Right to Life and Personal Liberty under Article 21.

# PART V: FAMILY, MATRIMONIAL & PERSONAL LAWS

## 1. MARRIAGE, DIVORCE & RESTITUTION (HINDU MARRIAGE ACT & SPECIAL MARRIAGE ACT)
*(Adjudicated Before Family Courts Established Under the Family Courts Act, 1984)*

### 1.1 Conditions of Marriage, Void & Voidable Marriages
* **Conditions for Valid Hindu Marriage (Section 5 HMA, 1955):** Neither party has a living spouse (monogamy), both are of sound mind and capable of valid consent, bridegroom has completed **21 years** and bride **18 years**, and parties are not within prohibited degrees of relationship or *sapindas* (unless permitted by custom).
* **Special Marriage Act, 1954 (Secular Civil Marriage):** Governs inter-faith and civil marriages regardless of religion, requiring a 30-day prior notice to the Marriage Officer and solemnization in the presence of three witnesses.
* **Void vs. Voidable Marriages (Sections 11 & 12 HMA):**
  * *Void Marriages (Sec. 11):* Bigamy, prohibited degrees, or *sapinda* relationship—void from inception (*void ab initio*).
  * *Voidable Marriages (Sec. 12):* Annulled by a decree of nullity on grounds of impotency, mental incapacity, consent obtained by force or fraud as to nature of ceremony/material fact, or pre-marriage pregnancy by another person (petition must be filed within **1 year** of discovery of fraud/cessation of force).

### 1.2 Restitution, Judicial Separation & Grounds for Divorce
* **Restitution of Conjugal Rights (Section 9 HMA / Section 22 SMA):** When either spouse has withdrawn from the society of the other without reasonable excuse, the aggrieved party may petition the Family Court for restitution of conjugal rights.
* **Fault Grounds for Divorce (Section 13(1) HMA / Section 27 SMA):** Either spouse may petition for dissolution of marriage on grounds of:
  1. **Adultery:** Voluntary sexual intercourse with any person other than his or her spouse.
  2. **Cruelty:** Treating the petitioner with physical or mental cruelty making it impossible to reasonably live together.
  3. **Desertion:** Abandonment of the petitioner for a continuous period of not less than **2 years** immediately preceding presentation of the petition without reasonable cause and without consent (*animus deserendi*).
  4. **Conversion:** Ceasing to be a Hindu by conversion to another religion.
  5. **Unsoundness of Mind:** Incurable unsoundness of mind or continuous/intermittent mental disorder of such a kind that the petitioner cannot reasonably be expected to live with the respondent.
  6. **Renunciation of World / Presumption of Death:** Entering a religious order or not being heard of as alive for **7 years or more**.
* **Additional Grounds Available Exclusively to Wife (Section 13(2) HMA):** Husband having another living wife (pre-1955 marriages), husband guilty of rape/sodomy/bestiality post-marriage, non-resumption of cohabitation for 1 year after a maintenance decree/order, or marriage solemnized before attaining age 15 and repudiated before attaining age 18 (option of puberty).
* **Divorce by Mutual Consent (Section 13B HMA / Section 28 SMA):** Joint petition presented on the ground that parties have been living separately for **1 year or more**, have not been able to live together, and have mutually agreed that the marriage should be dissolved. Followed by a **6-month cooling-off period** (which the Supreme Court in *Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur* held is directory and can be waived by the Family Court if reconciliation is impossible).
* **Muslim Personal Law (Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939 & Muslim Women [Protection of Rights on Marriage] Act, 2019):** Instant Triple Talaq (*Talaq-e-Biddat*) is void and a cognizable criminal offence punishable with imprisonment up to 3 years; a Muslim woman may obtain judicial divorce (*Faskh*) on 9 statutory grounds under the 1939 Act or via *Khula* / *Mubaraat*.

## 2. MAINTENANCE & ALIMONY (BNSS SECTION 144 & PERSONAL LAWS)

### 2.1 Secular Summary Maintenance (Section 144 BNSS — Replaces Section 125 CrPC)
* **Who Can Claim:** Applicable to all persons regardless of religion if any person having sufficient means neglects or refuses to maintain:
  * His **wife** (including a divorced woman who has not remarried) unable to maintain herself;
  * His legitimate or illegitimate **minor child** (whether married or not);
  * His legitimate or illegitimate **major child** (not being a married daughter) who by reason of physical or mental abnormality/injury is unable to maintain itself;
  * His **father or mother** unable to maintain himself or herself.
* **Interim Maintenance & Mandatory Disclosure:** Magistrate/Family Court may order monthly interim maintenance and litigation expenses during pendency of proceedings (to be decided within **60 days** of service of notice). Per *Rajnesh v. Neha* (2021), both parties must file a standardized **Affidavit of Disclosure of Assets and Liabilities**.

### 2.2 Maintenance Under Hindu Personal Laws
* **Maintenance Pendente Lite & Permanent Alimony (Sections 24 & 25 HMA):** Either spouse having no independent income sufficient for support and necessary expenses may claim interim maintenance during any HMA proceeding (Sec. 24) and permanent alimony/maintenance at or after passing any decree (Sec. 25).
* **Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act (HAMA), 1956 (Sections 18–20):** Entitles a Hindu wife to live separately without forfeiting her right to maintenance if the husband is guilty of desertion, cruelty, having another living wife, keeping a concubine, or religious conversion (Sec. 18), and obligates children to maintain aged/infirm parents (Sec. 20).

## 3. PROTECTION OF WOMEN FROM DOMESTIC VIOLENCE ACT (PWDVA), 2005
*(Civil Remedies with Quasi-Criminal Enforcement Before Judicial Magistrate)*

* **Broad Definition of "Domestic Violence" (Section 3):** Encompasses any act, omission, or conduct that harms, injures, or endangers health, safety, life, limb, or well-being—expressly covering four categories: (1) **Physical Abuse**, (2) **Sexual Abuse**, (3) **Verbal and Emotional Abuse**, and (4) **Economic Abuse** (deprivation of financial resources, household necessities, *stridhan*, or alienation of shared assets).
* **Aggrieved Person & Domestic Relationship (Section 2(a) & (f)):** Protects any woman who is, or has been, in a domestic relationship with the respondent (covering wives, live-in partners in relationships in the nature of marriage, mothers, sisters, and daughters).
* **Five Key Magistrate Reliefs (Sections 18–22):**
  1. **Protection Orders (Sec. 18):** Prohibiting the respondent from committing domestic violence, communicating with the aggrieved person, or alienating bank accounts/lockers/*stridhan*.
  2. **Residence Orders & Right to Shared Household (Secs. 17 & 19):** Every woman in a domestic relationship has a statutory right to reside in the **shared household**, whether or not she has any right, title, or beneficial interest in it (affirmed in *Satish Chander Ahuja v. Sneha Ahuja*). Magistrate may restrain dispossession or direct the respondent to secure alternate accommodation.
  3. **Monetary Reliefs (Sec. 20):** Compensation for loss of earnings, medical expenses, loss of property, and maintenance.
  4. **Custody Orders (Sec. 21):** Temporary custody of children to the aggrieved person.
  5. **Compensation Orders (Sec. 22):** Damages for mental torture and emotional distress.
* **Breach of Protection Order (Section 31):** Breach of an interim or final protection order by the respondent is a **cognizable and non-bailable criminal offence** punishable with imprisonment up to **1 year**, or fine up to **₹20,000**, or both.

## 4. CHILD CUSTODY, GUARDIANSHIP & SUCCESSION LAWS

### 4.1 Guardianship & Custody (Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 & HMGA, 1956)
* **Paramount Consideration — Welfare of the Child:** Across all personal laws and Section 17 of the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890, the **welfare and best interest of the minor** is the supreme, overriding consideration—prevailing over technical parental claims.
* **Natural Guardians (Section 6 HMGA, 1956):** For a Hindu minor boy or unmarried girl, the father is the natural guardian and after him the mother—provided that **custody of a minor who has not completed the age of 5 years shall ordinarily be with the mother** (and per *Githa Hariharan v. RBI*, the mother can act as natural guardian even during the father's lifetime if he is indifferent or absent).

### 4.2 Succession & Inheritance (Hindu Succession Act, 1956 & Indian Succession Act, 1925)
* **Coparcenary Rights of Daughters (Section 6 HSA — Amended 2005):** By birth, a daughter of a coparcener becomes a **coparcener in her own right** in the exact same manner as a son, having the same rights and liabilities in ancestral/joint family property regardless of whether she was born before or after 2005 (*Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma*).
* **Intestate Succession (Sections 8–16 HSA):**
  * *Hindu Male Dying Intestate (Sec. 8):* Property devolves first upon **Class I Heirs** simultaneously (son, daughter, widow, mother, children of predeceased son/daughter).
  * *Hindu Female Dying Intestate (Sec. 15):* Property devolves first upon sons, daughters (including children of any predeceased son/daughter), and the husband.
* **Testamentary Succession — Wills & Probate (Indian Succession Act, 1925):** Any person of sound mind (not a minor) may dispose of self-acquired property by a Will executed in writing, signed by the testator, and attested by **at least two witnesses** (Sec. 63). A Will can be revoked or altered at any time during the testator's lifetime.

# PART VI: SPECIALIZED TRIBUNALS & STATUTES (EVERY OTHER COURT FORUM)

## 1. CYBER LAW & DATA PROTECTION (IT ACT, 2000 & DPDP ACT, 2023)

### 1.1 Information Technology Act, 2000 (Amended 2008)
* **Legal Recognition of Electronic Records & Digital Signatures (Sections 4–5):** Grants full legal validity to electronic records and electronic/digital signatures.
* **Civil Compensation for Data Theft & Negligent Data Security (Sections 43 & 43A):**
  * *Sec. 43:* Any person who without permission accesses, downloads, introduces viruses/malware, damages, or disrupts a computer system is liable to pay damages by way of compensation to the affected person.
  * *Sec. 43A:* A body corporate handling sensitive personal data that is negligent in implementing reasonable security practices causing wrongful loss/gain is liable to pay compensation. Adjudicated by the State IT Secretary (Adjudicating Officer) with appeals to **TDSAT** within 45 days.
* **Cyber Criminal Offences (Sections 65–67B):**
  * *Tampering with Computer Source Documents (Sec. 65):* Up to 3 years imprisonment and/or ₹2 Lakh fine.
  * *Computer-Related Offences / Hacking (Sec. 66):* Dishonestly or fraudulently committing any act under Sec. 43 (up to 3 years and/or ₹5 Lakh fine).
  * *Identity Theft (Sec. 66C) & Cheating by Personation Using Computer Resource (Sec. 66D):* Covers phishing, OTP fraud, fake social media profiles, and impersonation scams (up to 3 years and ₹1 Lakh fine).
  * *Violation of Privacy (Sec. 66E):* Capturing, publishing, or transmitting the image of a private area of any person without consent (up to 3 years and/or ₹2 Lakh fine).
  * *Cyber Terrorism (Sec. 66F):* Punishable up to **imprisonment for life**.
  * *Publishing Obscene / Sexually Explicit Material & CSAM (Secs. 67, 67A, 67B):* Graded imprisonment from 3 to 7 years and heavy fines.
* **Intermediary Safe Harbour Immunity (Section 79):** An intermediary (social media platform, ISP, cloud host) is exempt from liability for third-party content only if: (1) its function is limited to providing access/hosting, (2) it does not initiate transmission, select the receiver, or modify the information, and (3) it observes statutory due diligence (IT Rules, 2021) and **expeditiously removes unlawful content upon receiving actual knowledge** (court order or government notification; *Shreya Singhal v. Union of India*).

### 1.2 Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023
* **Consent & Notice Mandate (Sections 5–6):** Data Fiduciaries (companies/platforms processing digital personal data) must provide a clear itemized notice and obtain **free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous consent** via clear affirmative action before processing personal data. Data Principals have the right to withdraw consent as easily as it was given.
* **Rights of Data Principals (Sections 11–14):** Right to access information about personal data processed, right to correction, completion, updating, and **erasure of personal data**, right of grievance redressal, and right to nominate another person in case of death or incapacity.
* **Protection of Children's Data (Section 9):** Mandatory verifiable parental consent before processing personal data of any child under **18 years**; strict prohibition on tracking, behavioural monitoring, or targeted advertising directed at children.
* **Penalties (Section 33 & Schedule):** The Data Protection Board of India can impose financial penalties up to **₹250 Crore** for failure to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach, and up to **₹200 Crore** for breach of children's data obligations or failure to notify data breaches.

## 2. MOTOR ACCIDENT CLAIMS TRIBUNAL (MOTOR VEHICLES ACT, 1988)
*(Adjudicated Before Special Motor Accident Claims Tribunals — MACT)*

* **Application for Compensation (Section 166):** May be filed by the injured person, the owner of damaged property, or all/any of the legal representatives of the deceased (where death resulted from the motor vehicle accident). Can be filed before the MACT having jurisdiction over: (a) the area where the accident occurred, (b) where the claimant resides or carries on business, OR (c) where the defendant resides.
* **No-Fault Liability (Section 164 — Amended 2019):** Owner of the motor vehicle or authorized insurer is liable to pay fixed compensation without requiring the claimant to plead or establish wrongful act, neglect, or default: **₹5 Lakhs** in case of death, and **₹2.5 Lakhs** in case of grievous hurt.
* **Hit-and-Run Compensation Scheme (Section 161):** Where the offending vehicle cannot be identified, statutory solatium is paid from the Central Motor Vehicle Accident Fund (**₹2 Lakhs** for death; **₹50,000** for grievous hurt).
* **Standardized Computation of Compensation (*National Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Pranay Sethi*, 2017):**
  * **Loss of Dependency:** Calculated using `(Annual Income + Future Prospects) - Personal Expenses × Age Multiplier` (per *Sarla Verma* table). Future prospects addition: **40%** (under age 40, self-employed/fixed salary) or **50%** (permanent job under age 40).
  * **Conventional Heads:** Loss of Estate (₹15,000), Funeral Expenses (₹15,000), and Loss of Consortium — spousal, parental, and filial (₹40,000 per claimant, subject to 10% enhancement every 3 years).
* **Third-Party Insurance & "Pay and Recover" Doctrine:** Under Section 146, third-party motor insurance is mandatory. Even if the insured owner breaches policy conditions (e.g., invalid driving license or permit violation), the insurer must first satisfy the award to the third-party victim and may subsequently recover the amount from the vehicle owner (*National Insurance Co. v. Swaran Singh*).
* **Appeals (Section 173):** Lies to the High Court within **90 days** from the MACT award (requires mandatory deposit of **₹25,000 or 50%** of the awarded amount, whichever is less; no appeal lies if the amount in dispute is less than ₹1 Lakh).

## 3. LABOUR, EMPLOYMENT & INDUSTRIAL DISPUTES LAWS
*(Adjudicated Before Labour Courts, Industrial Tribunals & Controlling Authorities)*

### 3.1 Termination, Retrenchment & Industrial Disputes (Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 / Industrial Relations Code)
* **Who is a "Workman"? (Section 2(s)):** Any person employed in any industry to do manual, unskilled, skilled, technical, operational, clerical, or supervisory work for hire or reward (excluding persons employed mainly in a managerial or administrative capacity, or supervisors drawing wages exceeding ₹10,000/₹18,000 per month—whose termination disputes are governed by civil contract law).
* **Conditions Precedent to Retrenchment (Section 25F):** No workman who has been in continuous service for not less than **1 year** (240 days) shall be retrenched by an employer unless:
  1. Given **1 month's written notice** indicating reasons for retrenchment (or paid wages in lieu of notice);
  2. Paid **retrenchment compensation** equivalent to **15 days' average pay for every completed year of continuous service** (or any part thereof in excess of 6 months); and
  3. Notice in the prescribed manner is served on the appropriate Government.
  *(Non-compliance renders termination void ab initio, entitling the workman to reinstatement with back wages).*
* **Domestic Inquiry & Natural Justice:** Before dismissing an employee for misconduct, the employer must issue a clear Charge-Sheet, hold an impartial domestic inquiry affording opportunity of defence (*Audi Alteram Partem*), and impose proportionate punishment.

### 3.2 Wages, Gratuity, Provident Fund & Workplace Harassment
* **Payment of Wages & Minimum Wages:** Employer cannot make unauthorized deductions from wages; unpaid wages can be recovered through the Authority under the Payment of Wages Act / Code on Wages with compensation up to 10 times the deducted amount.
* **Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 (Section 4):** Payable to every employee (in establishments with 10 or more employees) upon superannuation, retirement, resignation, death, or disablement after rendering continuous service for **not less than 5 years** (5-year rule waived in case of death or disablement). Calculated at `15 days' last drawn wages × completed years of service`.
* **Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) & Maternity Benefit:**
  * Mandatory EPF contribution (12% employer + 12% employee) for establishments with 20+ employees.
  * **Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (Amended 2017):** Entitles women employees (who have worked at least 80 days in the preceding 12 months) to **26 weeks of paid maternity leave** (for the first two children; 12 weeks thereafter), work-from-home options, and mandatory crèche facilities in establishments with 50+ employees. Dismissal during maternity absence is strictly illegal (Sec. 12).
* **Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (POSH) Act, 2013:**
  * Every employer with **10 or more employees** must constitute an **Internal Committee (IC)** headed by a senior woman employee and including an external NGO/legal member.
  * Written complaint must be filed within **3 months** of the incident (extendable by 3 months); IC must complete inquiry within **90 days**, and employer must act on recommendations within **60 days**.

## 4. CORPORATE LAW & INSOLVENCY RESOLUTION (COMPANIES ACT, 2013 & IBC, 2016)
*(Adjudicated Before the National Company Law Tribunal [NCLT] & Appellate Tribunal [NCLAT])*

### 4.1 Companies Act, 2013 — Oppression, Mismanagement & Class Actions
* **Separate Legal Entity & Lifting the Corporate Veil:** Upon incorporation, a company becomes a distinct juristic person separate from its shareholders (*Salomon v. Salomon*). Courts lift the corporate veil where the corporate structure is used for fraud, tax evasion, or circumventing statutory obligations.
* **Oppression and Mismanagement (Sections 241–242):** Any member(s) holding at least **10% of issued share capital** (or 1/10th of total members, or 100 members, whichever is less) may apply to the **NCLT** complaining that the affairs of the company are being conducted in a manner prejudicial to public interest, oppressive to any member(s), or prejudicial to the interests of the company. NCLT has wide powers to regulate company conduct, set aside fraudulent transfers, or order buyout of shares.
* **Class Action Suits (Section 245):** Empowers a group of shareholders or depositors to file a class action before the NCLT restraining the company/directors from ultra vires/fraudulent acts and claiming damages/compensation from directors, auditors, or experts for misleading statements.

### 4.2 Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016
* **Minimum Default Threshold (Section 4):** Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) can be triggered upon a default of at least **₹1 Crore**.
* **Three Ways to Initiate CIRP (Sections 7, 9, 10):**
  1. **Financial Creditor (Sec. 7):** Banks, NBFCs, debenture holders, or homebuyers (requires at least 100 homebuyers or 10% of total allottees in a real estate project, whichever is less) file upon default of a financial debt.
  2. **Operational Creditor (Sec. 8 & 9):** Suppliers of goods/services or employees claiming unpaid dues must first serve a **10-day Statutory Demand Notice**; if the corporate debtor neither pays nor demonstrates a **pre-existing dispute** within 10 days, the operational creditor may file before NCLT.
  3. **Corporate Applicant (Sec. 10):** The corporate debtor itself may initiate voluntary insolvency upon default.
* **Moratorium (Section 14):** Upon admission of the petition by NCLT, an immediate statutory **Moratorium** freezes all pending suits, execution proceedings, arbitration, and recovery/enforcement of security interests against the corporate debtor while the Resolution Professional (RP) and Committee of Creditors (CoC) evaluate resolution plans.
* **Strict Timeline & Liquidation (Sections 12 & 33):** CIRP must be completed within **180 days** (extendable once by 90 days, with an outer statutory cap of **330 days** including litigation time). If no resolution plan is approved by **66% voting share** of the CoC, the NCLT orders liquidation of the corporate debtor, distributing assets according to the **Section 53 Waterfall Mechanism** (insolvency costs $\rightarrow$ workmen's dues for 24 months & secured creditors $\rightarrow$ employees' dues $\rightarrow$ unsecured financial creditors $\rightarrow$ government dues $\rightarrow$ operational creditors $\rightarrow$ shareholders).

## 5. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS (IP) & ENVIRONMENTAL LAWS (NGT)

### 5.1 Intellectual Property Statutes (Commercial Courts / High Court IP Divisions)
* **Trade Marks Act, 1999:** Protects distinctive brand names, logos, slogans, and trade dress for **10 years** (renewable indefinitely every 10 years). Provides statutory **Infringement** action for registered marks (Sec. 29) and common-law **Passing Off** action for unregistered marks based on goodwill, misrepresentation, and damage (Sec. 27(2)).
* **Copyright Act, 1957:** Automatic protection upon creation (registration is optional but serves as *prima facie* evidence) for original literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic works, software code, cinematograph films, and sound recordings. Term: **Lifetime of author + 60 years** (60 years from publication for films/sound recordings/corporate works). Subject to **Fair Dealing** exceptions under Section 52 (private research, criticism, review, reporting current events, educational instruction).
* **Patents Act, 1970:** Protects novel, non-obvious inventions capable of industrial application for **20 years** from the date of filing. Excludes business methods, algorithms/computer programs *per se* (Sec. 3(k)), mere discovery of new forms of known substances without enhanced efficacy (Sec. 3(d)), and methods of medical treatment.

### 5.2 Environmental Litigation — National Green Tribunal (NGT) Act, 2010
* **Jurisdiction (Sections 14–16):** NGT has specialized jurisdiction over all civil cases where a **substantial question relating to the environment** (including enforcement of any legal right relating to environment) is involved, arising out of 7 core statutes: Water Act (1974), Air Act (1981), Environment (Protection) Act (1986), Forest (Conservation) Act (1980), Public Liability Insurance Act (1991), Biological Diversity Act (2002), and Water Cess Act.
* **Three Guiding Principles (Section 20):** NGT mandates adjudication based on: (1) **Sustainable Development**, (2) **Precautionary Principle** (lack of full scientific certainty cannot postpone measures to prevent environmental degradation), and (3) **Polluter Pays Principle** (polluter bears full cost of environmental remediation and victim compensation).
* **Limitation & Appeals (Sections 14(3), 16 & 22):** Original applications must be filed within **6 months** from when the cause of action first arose (extendable by 60 days); appeals against environmental clearances/orders within **30 days** (extendable by 60 days). Appeal from NGT lies directly to the **Supreme Court** within **90 days**.

## 4. GLOSSARY OF ESSENTIAL LATIN LEGAL MAXIMS

* ***Actus Non Facit Reum Nisi Mens Sit Rea*****:** An act does not make a person guilty unless the mind is also guilty (foundational principle of criminal intent).
* ***Audi Alteram Partem*****:** Hear the other side (core pillar of natural justice; no person shall be condemned unheard).
* ***Caveat Emptor*****:** Let the buyer beware (historically governed sales, now heavily curtailed by strict product liability and consumer rights under CPA, 2019).
* ***De Minimis Non Curat Lex*****:** The law does not concern itself with trifles (codified in BNS Section 33).
* ***Doli Incapax*****:** Incapable of committing a crime (irrebuttable presumption for children below 7 years under BNS Section 20).
* ***Ex Turpi Causa Non Oritur Actio*****:** From a dishonourable/illegal cause, no right of action arises (codified in Section 23 of the Indian Contract Act).
* ***Ignorantia Facti Excusat, Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat*****:** Ignorance of fact excuses, ignorance of law does not excuse (codified in BNS Sections 14 and 17).
* ***Lis Pendens*****:** A pending legal action (codified in Section 52 of the Transfer of Property Act, prohibiting transfer of disputed immovable property during litigation).
* ***Nemo Judex In Causa Sua*****:** No one should be a judge in their own cause (rule against bias; codified in Section 12(5) & Seventh Schedule of the Arbitration Act).
* ***Pacta Sunt Servanda*****:** Agreements must be kept (binding sanctity of valid contracts).
* ***Qui Facit Per Alium Facit Per Se*****:** He who acts through another does the act himself (principle of agency and vicarious liability under Contract Act Sec. 226).
* ***Res Gestae*****:** Things done; spontaneous acts and declarations forming part of the same transaction (codified in BSA Section 4).
* ***Res Ipsa Loquitur*****:** The thing speaks for itself (rule of evidence shifting burden in cases of obvious negligence, such as medical or motor accidents).
* ***Res Judicata*****:** A matter already judged (bars re-litigation of finally decided issues under CPC Section 11).
* ***Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium*****:** Where there is a right, there is a remedy (foundation of civil tort and statutory consumer actions).
